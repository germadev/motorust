# Architecture

How the code is organised and why. To build and run it see [building.md](building.md); the tracks and the bikes have their own pages: [tracks.md](tracks.md) and [models.md](models.md).

## Overview

motorust is a [Bevy](https://bevy.org) 0.19 app built from one plugin per area. `src/main.rs` loads the saved settings (they decide how the window opens), adds Bevy's `DefaultPlugins` without its gilrs plugin (gamepads are read by `src/gamepads.rs`), fixes the simulation rate and adds the game's plugins: world, bike, chase camera, gamepads, HUD, menu, minimap, welcome screen, track selection, pause, options, settings, sound and UI, plus the web plugin when compiled for `wasm32`.

- **Screens.** `GameState` (`src/menu.rs`) goes Welcome → Modes (choose what to play, `src/modes.rs`) → Tracks (choose a track) → Select (choose a bike and rider) → Race, with a Lobby before Modes for two players. The arcade championship skips Tracks: its tracks come in order, from the first one. After a race that ends, its results lead to the next race of the championship, back to Tracks, or back to Modes. The welcome screen, the lobby and the modes share one flight over the track, with no camera glide between them. Each track is built once and kept while choosing: entering Tracks prepares the others on background threads (track, terrain and its meshes), then adds them to the world hidden, one per frame, and leaving Tracks drops the ones not chosen. With the track seen from above while it is chosen (Options > Game), moving between screens slides the camera instead of cutting; seen from the road (the default), the flight goes on into the track screen and the camera cuts between it and the bikes.
- **Fixed-step physics, interpolated rendering.** Physics runs in `FixedUpdate` at **120 Hz**, at most 8 steps a frame (`MAX_STEPS_PER_FRAME`: after a late frame the game's time goes on by 8 steps and no more, so it keeps to real time down to 15 fps instead of jumping on up to Bevy's quarter of a second): `ai::drive_rivals` → `simulate_bikes` → `separate_bikes`, chained. Before the fixed loop the player's input is read; after it, `interpolate_bikes` places each bike between its previous and current state for the frame being drawn.
- **Pure, tested logic.** The parts that decide how the game behaves are plain functions with no window, renderer or ECS dependency, so `cargo test` exercises them headless: `bike::step` (one physics step), `bike::hit_walls` (walls), `bike::crash` (contacts between bikes, falls and rejoining the track), `bike::chassis` (wheelie and suspension springs), `bike::loop_ride` (riding round a loop), `Track` (the circuit and its queries), `ai::decide` (a rival's controls) and `pilot::pose` (the rider's race pose from its skeleton).
- **Units and axes.** Everything is in metres. The ground plane is Bevy's XZ plane with Y up; ground positions are `Vec2(x, z)` and a heading of 0 faces -Z, with positive values turning left (`src/geometry.rs`). Bike-local space is X right, Y up, -Z forward, with the origin on the ground between the wheels.
- **No external files at runtime.** Models, textures, the font and the music are embedded in the executable, and every sound effect is synthesised in real time.

## Code map

| Path | What it does |
|---|---|
| `src/main.rs` | Builds the app, registers the plugins and sets the simulation's clocks (`simulation_clocks`: 120 Hz, at most 8 steps a frame). |
| `src/geometry.rs` | Ground-plane conventions (`Vec2(x, z)`, heading) and shared numeric helpers (noise, seeded randomness, smoothing). |
| `src/track.rs` | The circuit layout: centreline (a closed Catmull-Rom curve through control points), elevation, banking and queries (nearest point, height, slope, grid slots). Where the track passes over itself, the queries for something on it (`nearest_at`, `road_height_at`) take its height and answer for the stretch at its level. Also the branches: open roads off the lap (`side_roads`, numbered in `Nearest::road`) that every query also looks at, lying on the lap's surface while they share its road and climbing or dropping smoothly to their loop once apart, and the vertical loops between them (`Loop`). What the ground beside the asphalt is, per point and side (`Surface`: grass, dirt with the colour of its dust, or paved), set from the world (`set_shoulders`). How far the walls stand from the centre line is the layout's (`Layout::wall`: 11.5 m, `WALL_DISTANCE`, on most tracks, less on a narrow one), closer where they close in (`narrows`), the asphalt half a metre short of them where they are nearer than its edge; the roads off the lap are no wider. Knows nothing about rendering. |
| `src/circuits/` | The tracks: `bay.rs` (Bahía GP, "the Bay"), `turbo_bay.rs` (Turbo Bay), `loop_island.rs` (Loop Island), `gold_mine.rs` (Gold Mine), `ghost_island.rs` (Ghost Island), `moon_base.rs` (Base Lunar, "Moon Base"), `fellowship_road.rs` (Fellowship Road) and `toy_store.rs` (Juguetería, "Toy Store"), each with its layout and scenery, and `mod.rs`, which lists them and tracks the one in use. See [tracks.md](tracks.md). |
| `src/world/mod.rs` | The world around the road for the chosen track; rebuilt when the track changes. A circuit is prepared away from the main thread, so putting it in the world only makes entities, in two parts: first what it is shown with (`Prepared`, `build`: its terrain and the terrain's meshes, the map of its shore and what lies beside its roads, the meshes of its roads, tunnels and bridges, its lakes, its signs, and the sea mirroring only the sky, `mirror::sky_mirror`), then the rest (`Rest`, `finish`, from the first part's `Basis`: the sea's map of what stands round it, its grass, where its trees and flowers go, put in the world with its houses and props, and then given to the sea's material with them standing). In the menus, from the welcome screen on (`preparing_in_the_menus`; on the web only in the tracks menu, as the background work runs on the main thread there), the others are prepared in the background, one piece of work at a time on a thread of their own (`circuit_pool`, `queue_circuits`): seen from the road, a quick look at the start of each first (`preview.rs`, `Previewing`, `PreviewRoots`, put in the world by `build` marked `PreviewRoot`, with the arches on its stretch and no signs), then the first part of each, the nearest in the menu to the one chosen first (`menu_distance`), then the rest of each, and last, seen from the road, a look at the start of any built with none (the first, built at start-up); nothing new while the chosen one is worked on now. One chosen there before it is shown is not waited for (`CircuitLoading`): its look at the start shown if it has one; if its work has not begun, it starts at once on another thread with every core, after a look at its start if it has none (`urgent_pool`, `Work::start`); its first part is shown, its roadside given to the track, when it comes, in place of its look at the start (hidden, not despawned), and its rest started at once (`spawn_prepared_circuit`, the chosen one first). Going on to a race, the others go (`unload_unused_circuits`), but not their looks at the start, kept hidden while the tracks menu is seen from the road (15 to 40 MB each, about 125 MB all five), so that after the race the menu shows any circuit's start at once while it is prepared again; the one chosen is made whole, its rest waited for (`complete_the_circuit`); elsewhere (a championship going on to the next track) the whole of it is waited for (`complete_now`). |
| `src/world/shoulders.rs` | What each road's shoulders are made of, worked out with the circuit: tunnel walkways and bridge decks are paved, a mine's galleries planked and its halls gravel or earth, elsewhere the terrain as drawn (grass, sand, bare earth). |
| `src/world/scenery.rs` | The data that describes a track's scenery: coast, hills, crags and rock walls, what lines the road (walls, tunnels, bridges, arches, signs) and the props, and the inside of a toy store (`ToyStore`). |
| `src/world/preview.rs` | A quick look at the start of a circuit for the tracks menu seen from the road, where the camera sets off from just before the line of each track chosen: from 120 m before the line to 450 m past it (`stretch`), its terrain fine and shaped by the roads only round that stretch and the land as it would be with no road elsewhere (`Terrain::shaped_within`), its roads cut to it (`cut_to`, triangle by triangle), its tunnels, bridges and arches on it (`structure_pieces` and `spawn_gantries` given the stretch), and the sea mirroring only the sky; worked out as a circuit's first part is (`prepare_preview`, a `Prepared`), a few times quicker (Gold Mine 0.9 s against 5.5 s, Ghost Island 3.4 s against 6.4 s in the test build on four cores). The lawns in flower are made once a session, each kind (`meadows`). |
| `src/world/terrain.rs` | Terrain with sea (or, inland, rivers and lakes), beaches, rock (or checkerboard earth), snow on the tallest mountains and bare scree under it, grass of each track's own greens (the arcade lawn; a Mediterranean island's straw, olive, sage and scrub in ragged patches; or a countryside's many greens: swathes, lusher by water, drier up high, forests up the hills and far away), as light as the track's `grass_shade`, grass in flower, hills, inlets and flat areas, computed once on a grid that is fine near the road and coarser towards the horizon (multi-threaded on the desktop); a triangle of the grid where the ground changes is cut along the border, found along its edges, so that the borders between grounds and between fields of flowers run smoothly instead of round whole triangles, in steps; the rock of the steep faces is told by the ground's slope smoothed over the grid (a little more or less here and there), so its borders run smoothly and raggedly too. The height of the water anywhere (`Terrain::water_level`: a dam's lake's level in it and on the rim round it, the sea's elsewhere), which the trees, houses, palms and flowers are planted clear over. Its meshes (`Terrain::meshes`) are made in bands of 24 rows of the grid dealt out to the threads in turn and joined in order, so they are the same however many threads make them. It is fine (3.5 m) and shaped by the roads round the whole circuit, or round a stretch of it only, the land elsewhere as it would be with no road (`Terrain::shaped_within`, for a quick look at a track's start, see `preview.rs`); the grass is coloured as the whole circuit's either way. |
| `src/world/sea.rs`, `src/world/sea.wgsl` | The sea, drawn one of two ways (`SeaWater`, changed at once by `dress_the_sea`): mirroring what stands round it, as a dam's lake (see `mirror.rs`; the map over the track and 700 m round it, at most 1024 texels a side and none under 2 m, a line of sight followed 1500 m), unless Options > Graphics > Water asks for the cartoon one ("Cartoon style") or the track always has it (`Scenery::cartoon_water`, Loop Island); or in the style of *The Wind Waker*: flat saturated blues with no lighting, drifting white wave lines, lighter water by the coast and foam rings rolling out from the shore. The shore comes from a map of the distance to land of each circuit, worked out in the background with the rest of the circuit. |
| `src/world/road.rs` | Asphalt, lines, kerbs in the corners, walls (stone, concrete, railings, hedges, checkerboard earth, timber or a shrine's vermilion fence) and the arches over the road (indoors, the road a toy track instead: a slot-car track's plastic with its slots and rails, Hot Wheels' orange track or a slide's chute, with their own borders, `toy_track`), following the track's height and banking, for the lap and the roads off it; where two roads meet, neither draws walls, lines or kerbs on the other. Walls are a cross-section swept along the road with rounded corners and shared vertices: coped stone, New Jersey barriers with joints, lumpy rounded hedges. |
| `src/world/tufts.rs` | Grass on the grass shoulders: tufts standing up, and a ragged edge lying over the edge of the asphalt; drawn only near the camera and without shadows. |
| `src/world/lamps.rs` | A few point lights that go to the tunnel lamps and mine lanterns nearest the cameras, fading in and out, so riders are lit as they pass under them. |
| `src/world/dam.rs` | Dams across gorges, as El Atazar's (see `Scenery::dams`): what they do to the ground (the river's bed and banks, the pool at the foot of the dam and the valley floor opening out below it, the gorge's far wall, the lake's bed and rim), worked out with the terrain; the arched concrete of the dam (its crest, as narrow as the walls over it, jutting out on brackets, its faces, streaked by the damp) and the square with its monument; and the lake's water, which mirrors what stands round it (`mirror.rs`, mapped 170 m round its shore every 2 m). |
| `src/world/mirror.rs`, `src/world/mirror.wgsl` | Water that mirrors what stands round it (`MirrorMaterial`), a dam's lake's and the sea's: unlit, dark (the sea and the lakes a reservoir's green-blue, a flooded pit's wine red), the reflected line of sight followed over a map of the heights and lit colours round the water (`surroundings_map`: the ground by what it is, the roads on it, the dams' crests; half floats, worked out with the circuit on several threads) until it meets the hills, the roads or a dam, or else taking the sky's colour that way (a map of the sky's gradient), mixed with the water's own colour by Fresnel and stirred by a faint ripple near the camera. What stands on the ground has maps of its own (`PreparedMirror::stand_up`, given as it is put in the world: the props of feudal Japan from points over their faces in their atlas's colours, and the crowns of the trees in colour, almond, momiji, ginkgo and sakura, from a few dozen of their vertices): over each texel, its lit colour, its top and its base, from where it is solid up over the water (what is under it, as a shrine's footing, left out), the texel looked at in half-metre bands, each in 4 × 4 parts, a band solid where it covers 3 of them or more with the bands either side (a floor, a wall, a roof), thin where it covers fewer (stilts, posts, seen through, so a shrine on stilts shows the water under its floor), leaves never thin; and how much of it the water shows (half as much for leaves). The texels round it take its colour and base with nothing standing, so its edge blends into nothing. The first thing standing the line of sight passes through on its way shows over what is behind it, faintly (at most 60%), broken up by patches drifting across the water. The line of sight is followed only where it is over the map, under the highest of it and as far as the water says, in as few steps as that stretch needs (8 to 64). |
| `src/world/structures.rs` | Tunnels cut through the rock (whose rock carries the road where the track passes over a tunnel, its gentle top grass, in flower as the fields round it where they are), with pools of lamplight drawn on their road and walls, bridges over the water (red arches or wooden) and billboards. |
| `src/world/props.rs` | Street lamps, trees and forests, palms, houses, tall hotels, the grand old hotel and buoys, placed pseudo-randomly but always the same way. Each tree is one opaque mesh of its trunk, branches and coarse lumpy crowns painted in its vertices, batched with the others of its variant, with two lighter meshes of the same shape and colours for farther away (the classic trees; each one is also planted with its modelled look, see `src/world/trees.rs`). |
| `src/world/trees.rs` | The trees modelled with Meshy (`assets/models/trees/`, prepared by `tools/import_trees.py`): one mesh of about four hundred triangles for each species and a fir for the forests, as tall as the classic ones, and one material with their colours in one texture; every tree planted with both looks (`Tree`) and dressed in the one Options > Graphics chooses, at once when it changes. Each look has three meshes, the full one and two lighter ones (`lighter`: a third and a tenth of its triangles, at most 200 and 60, the modelled ones painted in their vertices from their texture as it is seen from afar), and each tree is drawn with the one its distance from the nearest camera calls for (`dress_trees`, when Options > Graphics > "Lighter far trees" is on, off by default: from 100 m and 280 m, from 50 m and 150 m with the screen split), a few metres past each bound before it changes so it does not flicker. Its bounds are always its look's full mesh's. |
| `src/world/lod.rs` | Lighter meshes for afar (levels of detail): `simplify` collapses edges one by one, the cheapest first, each into a vertex where it best keeps the faces round it (quadric error metrics after Garland and Heckbert, area-weighted, with planes across open edges), never turning a face over, never leaving the mesh's box; the vertices at one spot are welded first so seams do not tear, the top of each part stays put (the tip of a cypress would otherwise sink along its own faces) and no part goes below two faces; colours averaged. `paint_from_texture` paints a textured mesh's colours in its vertices from a mip level; `level_at` picks the level for a distance. |
| `src/world/detail.rs` | How much detail the world is drawn with (`Detail`): the sun's shadows at a `Level` (High: four cascades out to 250 m, 2048 texels, as always; Medium: three out to 150 m; Low: two out to 90 m, 1024 texels), chosen in Options > Graphics or automatic (`AutoQuality`, a step down a `ladder` of `Rung`s at a `Pace`: in a race, not paused, every 2 s the frame rate against 60 fps or the cap; under 90 % a level down, at 97 % or more for 10 s a level up, that hold doubling, up to 5 min, each time a step up is undone within 20 s; 3 s let settle after every change, start or pause). The performance forced (Options > Graphics > "Force performance": off, 60 or 120 fps) uses the same with the shadows and the render scale together (`Detail::scale`, which `crate::render_scale`, the split screen's halves and the performance log follow): for one view eight rungs from shadows high at 100 % to low at 50 %, for two the render scale alone, 100 to 50 %; with the parallax chosen, a rung before them without it (`Detail::parallax`, `Ladder`, `PERFORMANCE_PARALLAX`, `PERFORMANCE_SPLIT_PARALLAX`; the relief shown then normal maps, `relief::capped`); every second against 60 or 120 fps, or fewer if the FPS limit lets fewer through (at the screen's rate, the screen's, `ScreenRate`: `performance_target`), under 95 % down as many rungs as it falls short by (`RUNG_GAIN`, three at most), 1 s let settle. The interface's camera loses its multisampling while the world's cameras draw into an image, told from their targets, so both change in the same frame. Only the shadows change with the level, so nothing pops. With the screen split (unless "Less detail for two players" is off) the shadows at Low, the grass, flowers and petals drawn 55 % as far (every `VisibilityRange` shortened, its full reach kept in `FullReach`) and the trees lighter sooner. The grass, flowers and petals fade out at the end of their reach (`fading_out`); on the web they end at once, halfway through the fade, as Bevy's fading fails on WebGL2 (see `docs/building.md`). The edges smoothed as chosen, fixed: MSAA 4× or 2× on every camera, or none on any, with SMAA or FXAA on the world's (`in_use`); 2× only where the GPU draws two samples a pixel of the cameras' colour and depth formats (`TwoSamples`, read from the adapter at startup: wgpu allows a count past one and four only with the adapter's own format features), offered in Options only there (`offered`) and drawn as 4× elsewhere; on the web, FXAA for SMAA. |
| `src/world/shadow_fade.rs` | Bevy ends the sun's shadows at the far bound of its last cascade, a hard edge a shadow pops in at. Its shadow shader (`bevy_pbr::shadows`) is patched as it loads (`patch_shadow_shader`, on the shader asset's events) so that `fetch_directional_shadow` fades the shadow out over the last fifth of the last cascade; if the code is not as expected (another Bevy), it is left alone with a warning. |
| `src/bench.rs` | The benchmark (`make bench`, `--bench` or `MOTORUST_BENCH=1`): free ride on autopilot over the tracks asked for (`MOTORUST_BENCH_TRACKS`) or all, a lap with one player (at a fixed reference, then shadows medium and low, the ground out of the shadows (`GroundPiece`: what drawing each of its meshes, over the whole circuit, into every cascade costs), MSAA 2× (left out where the GPU has not got two samples, `leave_out`), SMAA, FXAA, no antialiasing, lighter far trees, the world drawn at 70 % and 50 % of the window's resolution, the relief off, kept at speed, with parallax and on the road, the lawn tiled plainly and stochastically) and one with two (less detail, all of it, less detail at 70 %, without relief and with the ground out of the shadows) for each, the settings taking turns all round the lap (`turn_of`: 0.5 s to settle, 2 s measured, each round in another order) until it has gone all round and each has been measured `MOTORUST_BENCH_SECONDS` (10), every setting tried once first unmeasured while the shaders compile, the game's CPU read over each turn, where on the lap it was slowest and fastest against each setting's average (`extremes`); the frame rate unlimited and the settings marked `transient` (never saved); what it is doing shown under the frame rate, with the pixels the world is drawn at (`resolution_said`); a table printed and written, named after when, the system, the CPU and the GPU (`perf_log::log_name`), the performance log beside it, then the game closes. |

## Design decisions

- **Input lag and frame rate, the player's choice.** Pipelined rendering (`main.rs`) draws each frame while the next one is simulated: more frames when the CPU holds them back, a frame more between the controls and the screen. It is on by default and can be turned off in Options > Graphics > Advanced, as can the frames that may wait for the GPU (`desired_maximum_frame_latency`: 1, 2 as in Bevy, or 3); both take effect at the next start (`settings::StartedWith`). With a fixed FPS cap the wait happens after the frame is shown and before the next one reads the controls (`settings::limit_frames`, in the render world), so it never sits between reading them and showing them; and the frame is handed over to be shown at the next refresh (Mailbox), not on the screen's beat (Fifo), which would hold a cap over the screen's rate down to it (a BC-250 in a gamescope session, its screen at 60 Hz, stayed at 60 with 120 chosen). The controls are read in `PreUpdate` (gamepads) and just before the fixed steps of the frame (`bike::read_player_input`).
- **Fixed-step simulation with interpolation.** Physics always advances in steps of 1/120 s (`FixedUpdate`) and rendering places the bike between the previous step and the current one. The bike handles the same at any frame rate, and the simulation is reproducible, which will later allow replays, ghosts and multiplayer.
- **Loops in their own coordinates.** The bike is a body on the ground plane with a height, so it cannot go upside down by itself. Round a loop it switches to a ride on the running surface (`BikeState::ride`): how far round it is, how far to the side and which way it points on the surface (`Ride::yaw`). Its position, height and pitch come from those, and the usual `bike::step` drives it, with gravity along its way, so it accelerates, brakes and steers as on the road; the turn moves it round and across the surface. The edges are walls (`loop_ride::hit_walls`), as on the road but with no falls. The surface carries the bike sideways as it goes round (the loop's `shift`), so riding straight on follows it out beside the mouth. On the loop the heading stays the loop's, and the bike is drawn turned by `Ride::yaw` about its own up (`BikeState::orientation`), which is down at the top; off the loop the yaw goes back into the heading. That keeps the loop exact and cheap, and the rest of the physics untouched.
- **Arcade physics, not realistic.** The bike moves as a single body with a speed and a heading; it does not simulate balancing on two wheels. Turning is limited by a minimum radius at low speed and by lateral grip at high speed. Lean is purely visual and follows how much of the available grip the corner uses: little in gentle bends, full lean only at the limit (the angle that would balance the corner would lay the bike down with very little steering, given the arcade grip). The parameters live in `BikeTuning`.
- **Wheelies and suspension as springs.** The wheelie, the suspension pitch and its compression are three damped springs that advance with the fixed-step physics (`bike::chassis`). Pulling back, accelerating, braking or landing only changes where each spring pulls or gives it a kick, and the spring does the rest: it rises slowly, drops quickly, bounces and settles. The acceleration that moves the suspension, the rider and the camera is the one the rider would feel, without the slope's: rolling downhill off the throttle does not push you back.
- **Pure logic separated from the ECS.** `bike::step`, the collision functions, `Track`, `ai::decide` and the rider pose do not depend on windows or rendering, so they have tests (`cargo test`) and are shared: the rivals drive with exactly the same physics as the player.
- **Shared model blueprint.** A bike is a list of parts (`BikeBlueprint`), each with a mesh, a paint and the point of the bike it is attached to (chassis, steering, front or rear wheel). Meshes are created once and every bike reuses them, changing only its body and its livery, so adding rivals is almost free.
  - The code-built bodies are made of lofts: cross-sections along each part. The bodywork uses polygonal sections joined by flat faces with flat shading (`shapes::faceted`), the low-poly style of the design sheets; sections can be slanted to bevel the fairings. Each `Design` comes from measuring the side view of its sheet, scaled to the wheelbase.
  - The imported Meshy bikes plug into the same mounts: the importer splits each one into chassis, steering and wheels (see [models.md](models.md)).
- **Skinned riders with inverse kinematics.** The code-built rider's body is a single mesh deformed by a ten-bone skeleton (pelvis, chest, upper arms, forearms, thighs and shins), so shoulders, elbows, waist and knees bend without seams. The Meshy riders use their own 23-bone auto-rigged skeleton. In both, arms and legs use two-bone inverse kinematics: the hands never let go of the grips while the torso moves, and the feet stay on the pegs while the rider hangs off and puts the knee down.
- **Decoupled input.** The bike reads a `BikeInput` and does not care where it comes from: the keyboard or a gamepad fill it for the player, and `ai::decide` fills it for each rival.
- **Self-contained executable.** Models, textures, the font and the music are embedded with `include_bytes!`, liveries are painted into textures at startup and sound effects are synthesised, so a single file runs the game, on the desktop and on the web alike.
- **Music streamed from memory.** Each song is decoded as it plays, straight from the embedded MP3, and loops by decoding it again from the start (`rodio`'s `LoopedDecoder`). Bevy's own looping keeps every decoded sample to replay it, about 60 MB for a 3-minute song.
