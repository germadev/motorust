### 1.2 What Was Found

**The CPU's 60 % means one of two things, depending on where it was read.**
- In Activity Monitor, 100 % is one core: 60 % is little more than half a core of ten, so the CPU does not hold the game back and the GPU (the pixels) does. This is what the mini always measured: on 2026-10-16 (v0.0.10, `bench-results/2026-10-16_1057_macos-26-5-1_apple-m4.*`) the simulation took about 0.4 ms a frame and the main world's update about 1.1 ms (`update_ms_p50`), the CPU at 8–9 %; the world drawn at 50 % gave +49–62 %, no antialiasing against MSAA 4× +23–47 %; two players at full detail about 53 fps.
- In the game's own performance log or benchmark (`cpu_process_pct`, the table's `cpu %`), the share is of all the cores together (`cpu_share` in `motorust-engine/src/perf_log.rs`): 60 % on the M4's ten cores is about six cores busy, against 9 % before. That would be a CPU regression, some system doing heavy work every frame, most likely in what came since: the hover bikes and anti-gravity track (engine v0.0.26, about 4,300 lines), the neon sea grid's shader (v0.0.25), clouds, props.
- Since 2026-10-16 the mini has not been measured: the relief of the textures, Fellowship Road, Base Lunar, Juguetería, Costa Bits, Puerto Neón and the hover bikes all came after.
- To tell which: the circuit and players where it happens, whether Bahía GP does it too, where the 60 % was read, and the performance log (`~/Library/Application Support/<game folder>/logs/`): `update_ms_p50` near 1 ms says GPU, near 25 ms says the main world's CPU; with `render=`, `antialiasing=`, `shadows=`. Quick check: the render scale at 70 % or FXAA; if the frame rate climbs a lot, it is the GPU.
- Not yet checked in the code: no per-frame hot spot was found in a first look (`light_the_sky` in `world/sky.rs` only rebuilds on a change of circuit). The benchmark will point at a track first.

**The benchmark from the installed binary.** `motorust --bench` (two dashes; `motorust bench` does nothing: `bench.rs` looks for the exact argument), or `MOTORUST_BENCH=1 motorust`. The results go to `bench-results/` in the folder it is run from, so: `mkdir -p ~/motorust-bench && cd ~/motorust-bench && caffeinate -dis motorust --bench` (`caffeinate` as `make bench` does, so the display does not sleep). `MOTORUST_BENCH_TRACKS=0,2` and `MOTORUST_BENCH_SECONDS=5` shorten it. From the `.app`, run `Motorust.app/Contents/MacOS/motorust --bench` from the terminal, never from Finder (it runs from `/`). Esc stops it, keeping the laps measured.

**Apex Neural Rendering (ANR): not to integrate.** Read from its README on 2026-11-19:
- Windows 10/11 and Linux/Proton only, D3D12 or Vulkan games on NVIDIA, AMD or Intel GPUs; nothing for macOS, Metal or Apple Silicon.
- Installed into finished games as a DLL replacement (`dxgi.dll`, over OptiScaler, GPL-3.0), not a library a game links against: a Rust/Bevy game on wgpu has nowhere to plug it in.
- Its source "coming soon", no licence found: it could not go into an MIT/Apache engine.
- Only frame generation is out (beta); the upscaler (ANR SR) is "beta soon".
- Frame generation makes in-between frames: 40 fps would look like about 80 but still answer the controls at 40, with more latency, which a racing game feels.

**Another engine: not now.** The engine is about 101,000 lines of Rust and WGSL and the game about 20,000 (physics at 120 Hz, the rivals' AI, circuits and terrain, sky, synthesised sound, menus, editor, updater, web); almost none of it carries over to Godot, Unity or Unreal, and the open engine just split off would have to be rethought. The measurements so far say the frame is bound by the pixels, not by Bevy, and a frame bound by its pixels is as slow in any engine with the same shadows, MSAA and resolution.
- At the same scene: Unreal 5 likely slower on a base M4 (Lumen and Nanite are costly on Metal); Godot 4 often less optimised than Bevy, its Metal backend recent (4.4); Unity URP the one that knows Apple Silicon best, about the same or a little better, not twice.
- What they would bring: good temporal upscaling (Unreal's TSR, Unity's STP/FSR, Godot's FSR 2 and, to confirm, MetalFX from 4.5), the one thing that could take the mini from 40 to 70–80; ready-made GPU profilers, automatic LODs, occlusion, static batching; a native Metal backend without wgpu's layer (which does not matter at 9 % CPU).
- What would be lost: the simulation, the AI and the hundreds of tests in Rust; months without the game moving.
- When it would make sense: the benchmark showing the time inside Bevy itself (its extraction or render world) with no way to cut it; something needed that Bevy lacks (MetalFX, DLSS/FSR, consoles); wanting a full visual editor. Then a one-circuit prototype in the other engine, compared by numbers, before rewriting anything.

**Another language: no gain.** Rust is native, without a garbage collector, with SIMD and threads, on a par with C and C++; C# (Unity) is slower and its collector can stutter; GDScript much slower (Godot's heavy work is C++, or Rust through `godot-rust`); Swift about the same, Apple only. The language only touches the CPU's work, under 1 ms a frame; the GPU runs WGSL shaders either way. Reasons to change would be an engine's, compile times (eased by `make up`'s dynamic linking) or hiring.

**Texture resolution and levels of detail.**
- The world's textures have mipmaps already (`generate_mipmaps` in `world/textures.rs`): far off the GPU reads small versions, so lowering their resolution saves memory, hardly frames. The anisotropic filter at 16× on the ground and the asphalt (`sampler`, `anisotropy_clamp: 16`) may cost on the M4: 4× or 8× is a one-line test, a few fps at most. The Meshy bikes' textures take about 300 MB (KTX2 would save memory, not frames).
- Levels of detail exist for the trees (`world/lod.rs`, quadric error collapse) and visibility ranges with fading for the grass, flowers and tufts (`world/detail.rs`). There are none for the Meshy props of Costa Bits and Puerto Neón, the structures, or the rivals' bikes and riders: dense meshes drawn whole in the main pass and in each shadow cascade (four at high), twice with two players. If the slow track is one of the new ones full of props, this is a strong suspect, on the GPU (vertices) and the CPU (draw calls). The ground, one mesh a kind over the whole circuit, is not culled either (`ROADMAP.md`, step 2).
- The performance log counts entities, not triangles nor draw calls: with those, the benchmark would say at once whether geometry is the cause.

**Hardware features Bevy does not use on Apple Silicon** (mostly wgpu's, not Bevy's, so not reachable from the game; details may have changed in recent wgpu releases and are to be confirmed):
- **Tile memory for MSAA and depth** (memoryless render targets): Apple's GPUs draw in tiles in fast on-chip memory, and Metal lets the MSAA 4× and depth targets live only there. As far as known wgpu does not expose it fully, so they are stored whole to memory: part of why MSAA 4× cost the mini 23–47 %. FXAA or SMAA, the game's default being FXAA, side-steps it.
- **MetalFX** (Apple's spatial and temporal upscaler): not in wgpu; the biggest gain possible on the mini, but it needs calling Metal by hand beside wgpu.
- **Multi-draw indirect:** Bevy's GPU-driven rendering batches many meshes into one call; Metal has no direct equivalent and wgpu emulates it with several, so on a Mac Bevy spends more CPU an object than on a PC: felt with many different props on screen.
- Little worth here: Variable Rasterization Rate (not in wgpu), half precision (f16) in shaders (wgpu allows it, Bevy's shaders are f32; small gain for much work), hardware ray tracing (Bevy Solari is Vulkan/DX12 only), mesh shaders (experimental).
- Already used: pipelined rendering, macOS Game Mode through the `.app`, the panel's own resolution rather than 4096 × 2304, instanced trees and grass, hardware filtering and mipmaps.

### 1.3 Next, on the M4 Mini (in this order)
1. `motorust --bench` (or `make bench` from the repository) at the current version, with the `.txt` and the `.perf.log`, compared with 2026-10-16's. Note where the 40 fps and the 60 % were seen (circuit, players, Activity Monitor or the game's log).
2. If CPU-bound (`update_ms` high, `cpu %` of all cores high): find the system with Tracy (`--features bevy/trace_tracy`) and fix it in the engine; the newest work first (hover bikes, the neon grid, Puerto Neón's props).
3. If GPU-bound: per track, which setting gives the difference. Then, cheapest first: the anisotropy at 4×; LOD for the Meshy props with `world/lod.rs`'s simplifier; the render scale below 100 % with Bevy's contrast-adaptive sharpening (CAS) on Macs; the ground in pieces (`ROADMAP.md`, step 2); Bevy's TAA as the ground for a temporal upscale; MetalFX beside wgpu only if all that is not enough.
4. Offered, not done: triangles and draw calls a frame in the performance log, before the benchmark.

## 2. The Drop Is Worst on Costa Bits (2026-11-19)

### 2.1 User Request
> "He notado la caída especialmente en Costa Bits" (the drop shows most on Costa Bits).

### 2.2 What Was Found in the Engine's Code (v0.0.26; not measured yet)
- **The voxel ground is one mesh of individual quads over the whole circuit, casting shadows.** `Terrain::voxel_row` (`motorust-engine/src/world/terrain.rs`) makes a top for every block and a side wherever a neighbour is lower; `voxel_quad` pushes six unshared vertices a quad (`Builder::push(None, …)`), each with position, normal, UV (always zero) and colour, 48 bytes. The rows are appended into a single `GroundMesh` (plus one for the verges), which, being opaque and lit, casts shadows (`shadowless` in `world/mod.rs` only for blended, additive or unlit materials). So it is neither culled by the cameras nor by the cascades: all of it is drawn in the main pass and in each shadow cascade (four at high), and again for each view with two players.
- **A rough estimate, to be confirmed by a count:** 1 m blocks within 35 m of the roads (`VOXEL_RINGS`), 2 m to 90 m, 4 m to 250 m, 8 m to the fine grid's edge (`FINE_MARGIN`, 260 m past the roads); over Costa Bits' 4.39 km lap and its fork, of the order of 700,000 blocks and, with their sides, over a million quads: a few million triangles and some 5–10 million vertices (hundreds of MB), drawn five times a frame at high shadows. On a base M4 (tile-based: every triangle is binned before it is shaded) this alone could explain 40 fps, and why the other tracks, with smooth ground of shared vertices, do better.
- **Less likely, but there:** the trees and palms as sprite cards (`world/sprites.rs`, `face_the_camera`) turn to the camera every frame, so all their transforms change and are propagated and uploaded again each frame; up to `MAX_TREES` (2,200) scattered plus the forests' and the palm rows': a CPU cost of a millisecond or two, not six cores. The Meshy props (`src/game/costa.rs`) have no levels of detail.

### 2.3 To Do (on the M4 mini, after the benchmark confirms it)
1. Measure: Costa Bits alone (`MOTORUST_BENCH_TRACKS` with its index), `1p` against `1p-no-ground-shadows` and the render scales; and a test or log line counting the voxel ground's triangles and vertices.
2. Cheapest fixes, in the engine:
   - Indexed quads, four shared vertices instead of six (a third fewer), and no UVs where the material has no texture.
   - Merged tops (greedy meshing): neighbouring blocks of the same height and colour as one quad; the 12 shades a channel leave runs to merge, above all far off.
   - The ground in pieces (`ROADMAP.md`, step 2), tiles of 100–200 m, so the cameras and the cascades draw only those they reach.
   - Only the near pieces casting shadows, or the far rings (4 m and 8 m) out of the shadows.
   - The rings tuned: the 1 m blocks to 20–25 m rather than 35 m.
3. Then the cards: turn them only when the camera has turned or moved enough, or face them in the vertex shader (no transform changed on the CPU).
