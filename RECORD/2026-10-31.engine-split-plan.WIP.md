# The Plan to Split the Engine from the Game (2026-10-31)

The whole plan to make Motorust's engine an open-source project of its own and keep the game (its characters, models, music and circuits) private: what the user decided, what is done, and what is left, step by step. `ROADMAP.md` ("The engine apart from the game") has the steps in short; this record is the plan in full, kept up to date as the work goes on.

## 1. The Decision

### 1.1 User Requests
- 2026-10-28 (`RECORD/2026-10-28.performance-review-follow-up.completed.md`, section 2): "Lo que busco con .ron son dos cosas: poder desarrollar un editor de mapas; separar el motor del juego, el motor será open source, y los personajes y assets, privados." (what I want from .ron is two things: to be able to build a map editor; and to split the engine from the game, the engine open source and the characters and assets private).
- 2026-10-31, while step 2 was under way: "Cuando terminemos con todo eso, el objetivo es separar el motor del juego. Para ello, inicialmente separaremos el motor en la carpeta motorust-engine/. Una vez hecho, convertiré esa carpeta en un repositorio público. Y finalmente, este proyecto lo pasaré a un repositorio privado, usando como dependencia motorust-engine" (when we are done with all that, the aim is to split the engine from the game. First we will split the engine into the folder `motorust-engine/`. Once that is done, I will make that folder a public repository. And finally I will move this project to a private repository, with `motorust-engine` as a dependency).
- 2026-10-31: "Deja registrado todo el plan de separación en RECORD" (record the whole separation plan in RECORD): this file.

### 1.2 The Aims
- **An open-source engine**, `motorust-engine`: everything that makes a motorbike racing game in Bevy, knowing nothing of this game's content, running on its own with open demo content.
- **A private game**, this project: the engine as a dependency, and its own characters, models, music, circuits and themed scenery.
- **A map editor**, in the engine: circuits laid out and dressed without writing code or compiling (step 5).

### 1.3 The Order the User Set
1. The engine apart in a folder of this repository, `motorust-engine/`, the game here depending on it (step 4).
2. That folder made a public repository, by the user (step 6).
3. This project moved to a private repository, `motorust-engine` its dependency (step 6).

## 2. What Is Done

- **Step 1, the circuits and the bikes as data** (`RECORD/2026-10-30.circuits-in-ron.completed.md`, `RECORD/2026-10-31.circuits-resource.WIP.md`; germadev/motorust#31, #32):
  - all eight circuits are RON files in `assets/circuits/`, read by `src/circuits/data.rs`;
  - the bikes' roster is `assets/bikes/roster.ron`;
  - both are read into resources as the game starts, `Circuits` and `Roster`, instead of `const` arrays the engine knew;
  - one bike for each place on the grid (`RIDERS`), checked as the file is read.
- **Step 2, the terrain's functions as shapes** (`RECORD/2026-10-31.terrain-shapes.WIP.md`):

  - one function is left for a file to name: the gullies of Ghost Island's volcano, the way out for what the game adds of its own.

## 4. What Is Open and What Is Private

- **Open (the engine's own demo content, so that it runs on its own):**
  - the four bikes built in code with their painted liveries (`Design::Afilada`, `Escudo`, `Alerones`, `Rombo`; `src/bike/model/livery.rs`);
  - the Poly Haven textures (CC0) and the NASA maps (public domain), credited in `assets/CREDITS.md`;
  - the UI font and the gamepads' art, if their licences allow (to check in `assets/CREDITS.md`);
  - a plain circuit, Bahía GP, without its song (the music is private): the engine must run a circuit with no song, or with a song of its own under an open licence.
- **Private (the game):**
  - the Meshy bikes and riders, all the music, the modelled trees, and the props and toys (feudal Japan, Middle-earth, the toy store);
  - the circuits after other works (Fellowship Road, Ghost Island) and the rest of the game's circuits;
  - the game's own roster, the start menu's art and the name and icon, if they are to stay the game's.

## 5. The Steps Left

### Step 3: A content pack through Bevy's asset server
- Circuits, roster, models, music and textures loaded as assets (a Bevy asset source), not built into the code.
- In the game's release, built in all the same (an embedded asset source), so that it stays a single executable, the web build too.
- In development, and in the editor, read from the disk and reloaded when they change.
- The circuits' and roster's files as asset types of their own, read by the loaders of `src/circuits/data.rs`. The model packs (`props.bin`, the bikes' parts) and the songs as assets.
- The engine then ships its open demo content as its own pack, and the game adds its pack over it.
- Suggested order, smallest first: the music (one place, `music`), the circuits and the roster (already files), the textures, the model packs, the bikes.

### Step 4: The workspace, `motorust-engine/` in this repository
- A Cargo workspace at the root, its members `motorust-engine/` (a library crate, `motorust_engine`) and the game (this crate, or a `game/` crate), the game depending on the engine by path: `motorust-engine = { path = "motorust-engine" }`.
- **Into `motorust-engine/`:**
  - the track (`src/track.rs`), the bikes' simulation, physics, crashes and riders' poses with the code-built designs and liveries (`src/bike/`, but for the imported models), the AI;
  - the race and its modes, the HUD and minimap, the menus, options, settings and locale, the gamepads, the sound engine (`src/sound/` but for the songs);
  - the world's generic parts: terrain, roads, walls, tunnels, bridges, dams, the sea, the sky, trees, grass, flowers, lamps, props in general, the shapes of the mountains and the water;
  - the circuits' and roster's data (`src/circuits/data.rs`, `Circuits`, `Roster`);
  - the benchmark, the tools that work on circuits (`tools/circuit_layout.py`);
  - the open demo content, and the tests of all of it.
- **Staying in the game:**
  - the circuits' files and their own tests;
  - the roster;
  - the Meshy models and their importers (`tools/import_*.py`);
  - the songs;
  - the themed scenery (feudal Japan, Middle-earth, space, the desert and the mine, the toy store, Loop Island's loops and rings);
  - Ghost Island's volcano's gullies;
  - `tools/toy_store_layout.py`.
- **The themed code goes to the game**:
  - as generic features of the engine where it can (a corridor of torii is a corridor of gates with a model; a prop pack is a prop pack);
  - otherwise through points the engine offers to hook into: plugins adding their own scenery, read from the circuit's file by a name the game registers (as `terrain_function` does for functions now). `Scenery`'s themed fields become entries the game's plugins read.
- **The names the engine knows of the game's content** become registrations:
  - the designs and models the roster can name;
  - the songs, the props' models;
  - the terrain functions.
- **CI:** a job building and testing the engine alone, with its open content only, so that nothing private creeps into it.
- **The engine's own docs and records** move with it: what of `docs/` is about the engine. `AGENTS.md`'s rules apply to both.

### Step 5: The editor, in the engine
- Inside the game's own world, so that what is edited is seen as it will be raced:
  - the plan and the height profile;
  - control points, heights, banking and stretches;
  - what stands along the road;
  - the shapes of the mountains and the water.
- The limits `tools/circuit_layout.py` and the tests check, shown as it edits, ported to Rust and shared with the tests: `curves_are_drivable`, `slopes_and_banking_stay_rideable`, the crests the bike takes off over.
- Only the road and the ground near a change rebuilt.
- It writes the circuit's file: comments the user wrote by hand are lost on a file the editor writes, so the notes that matter become fields.

### Step 6: The engine published, and the game private
- **The engine's repository**, made by the user: `motorust-engine/` becomes a public repository of its own.
  - It starts **a new history**, with the folder's files as its first commit, never this repository's history. This repository's history holds every model, song and circuit (a 99 MiB pack), and the engine's folder may hold some of it in its past.
  - Not `git subtree split` without checking: it keeps the history of the folder, and any private file ever committed there.
  - Before publishing: a search of the folder for anything private (models, songs, the game's circuits, their names), and its licence (MIT or Apache-2.0, as Bevy) and credits (`CREDITS.md` of its open content).
- **The game's repository**, private: this project moved there by the user, with `motorust-engine` a dependency:
  - first by its git repository and a tag, `motorust-engine = { git = "https://github.com/…/motorust-engine", tag = "v…" }` (or the crate on crates.io, if it is published there);
  - in development, the engine's checkout beside it by a `[patch]` or a path, so that both can be worked on at once.
  - The engine's version pins Bevy's: the game takes Bevy through the engine (re-exported), so the two never differ.
- **Afterwards**: the engine's changes go to its repository, and the game takes them by bumping its tag.

### 10.3 The Proposal: Seams First, Then the Move
Not a single move of files into a new crate: the themed modules are woven into the build, and a move would break everything at once. Instead, in this crate, the seams first, each a PR of its own with the tests green; then the move, mechanical.

**4a. What the code names becomes data** (small, at once):
- the Meshy bikes: each model's measures in a file of its folder (`assets/models/shogun/model.ron`), the roster naming a model by its folder; `imported.rs` keeps only the loader;
- the menus' song and the chill songs in `assets/music/music.ron`; the engine plays no song if there is none;
- the game's identity (title, the start menu's lettering, folders' names, icon) in a resource the game inserts (`GameInfo`).

**4b. Themes as plugins** (the big one, one theme at a time):
- a registry in the engine: `app.register_theme::<Japan>("japan")`; a circuit's file writes its themes in a map, `themes: { "japan": (...) }`, each read by the theme registered by that name (from a `ron::Value`), an unknown name refused as today;
- a `Theme` trait with the points the build calls: its pieces on the preparing thread (as `structures.rs` asks the toy store), what it spawns (as `props.rs` calls `spawn_japan`), its fences by the road (`prop_fence`); its model packs and animations its own plugin's resources and systems;
- the themes moved into `src/game/` one by one (the desert and the mine first, the smallest and least woven; the toy store last), each circuit's file rewritten with them;
- a test failing if anything outside `src/game/` names `crate::game`, so the seam holds.

**Stay in the engine as generic features** (the plan's "a corridor of torii is a corridor of gates"): Moon Base's realms and portals (`realm.rs`, woven into the sky, the sound and the render scale), the excavations and caverns (`cavern.rs`, in the terrain and the shoulders), the ridden loops, rings, totems, waterfalls and sunflowers (`landmarks.rs`, the loops ridden by `loop_ride.rs`), the boosters (in the bikes' physics), dams, the sea, the sky, trees, grass and flowers. The modelled trees' code too (a tree pack); its pack is the game's.

**4c. The engine's tests on its own content**:
- Bahía GP (without its song) as the engine's demo circuit, the tests that build a world or race on it;
- what the tests check on every circuit (drivable curves, rideable slopes, the crests, the world built) as helpers the engine exports, which the game's tests run on the game's circuits;
- the circuits' own tests (`fellowship_road.rs`, `gold_mine.rs`, …) with the game.

**4d. Two packs**: the content pack read from the game's `assets/` over the engine's (a file looked for in the game's first); each crate's `build.rs` builds its own in (twenty lines, copied: a build helper depending on the engine would compile Bevy for a build script).

**4e. The move**:
- a workspace at the root: the game stays the root package (`cargo run`, the Makefile, the web build and CI as they are), its member `motorust-engine/` (`motorust_engine`, a library);
- `git mv` of everything but `src/main.rs` and `src/game/` into `motorust-engine/src/`; the engine's items `pub` where the game uses them (broadly first, made narrower later); the engine's open assets (`circuits/bahia.ron`, the textures, the font, the gamepads' art) into `motorust-engine/assets/`;
- the game: `main.rs` (the engine's plugins, its themes, its identity), `src/game/`, `assets/` (the rest), its tests;
- CI: `cargo test -p motorust-engine` with its own assets only, so nothing private creeps in;
- the docs and the tools split (`tools/circuit_layout.py` with the engine; `toy_store_layout.py` and the importers with the game), `AGENTS.md` in both.
