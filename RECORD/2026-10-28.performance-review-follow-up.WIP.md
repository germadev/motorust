### 1.2 The Review (Answered in the Conversation)
- **Already done, from earlier records:** the measuring (performance log and `make bench`, four machines, `RECORD/2026-10-16.performance-plan-and-measuring-guide.completed.md`); `opt-level = 1` for the game's own code in dev, `lto = "thin"` and `codegen-units = 1` in release (the guide says `make up` does not optimise the game's own code: it does, at level 1); three shadow levels, automatic, faded at their far end; the antialiasing option; the nearest point on the road through a grid (`src/track/grid.rs`, 16–43× faster, the BC-250's long tracks from 64–70 to 175–189 fps). No HDR, bloom nor volumetric fog to cut (only `DistanceFog`). The bikes' 90–200 Meshy pieces are already merged by the importer into five parts (`tools/import_models.py`); the trees share a mesh and material a variant (instanced); about 3,700 entities alive in a race.
- **Against the measurements:** the guide puts the shadows first. On the M4 mini (`bench-results/2026-10-16_1057_*`, still at 4096 × 2304): the world at 50 % +49–62 %, no antialiasing +23–47 % (against MSAA 4×), FXAA instead +6–21 %, shadows low +9–26 %; the simulation 0.4–0.8 ms a frame. The Macs are bound by the pixels, not by Bevy.
- **Corrections to the first answer:** it said the game starts with MSAA 4× and normal maps; since `RECORD/2026-10-20.graphics-defaults-and-forced-performance-rows.WIP.md` it starts with FXAA and no relief. The benchmark keeps MSAA 4× and normal maps as its reference.
- **What is left from the guide:** measure again (no results since 2026-10-16, before the relief, Fellowship Road, Base Lunar and the new web, and before the M4 mini's render was capped at its panel's pixels); the ground, one mesh a kind of ground over the whole circuit (`GroundMesh`), so neither the frustum's culling nor the cascades' leave any of it out, drawn whole in the main pass and in each cascade, and again for each view with two players; Tracy for the render world's CPU time (pending in `RECORD/2026-10-16.m4-mini-follow-up.WIP.md`). KTX2 judged of little worth for the frame rate (the textures filling the screen are made at startup; the bikes' would save about 300 MB of memory), and GPU occlusion culling of little worth on Apple's tile-based GPUs (they already drop hidden opaque fragments; it needs a depth prepass).

### 1.3 Implementation Details
- **SMAA and MSAA 2×** (`settings.rs`, `world/detail.rs`, `options.rs`, `hud.rs`, `perf_log.rs`): `Antialiasing::Msaa2` (two samples a pixel) and `Antialiasing::Smaa` (Bevy's `Smaa`, its default preset, high), between MSAA 4× and FXAA in Options > Graphics (`ANTIALIASINGS`); saved as `Msaa2` and `Smaa`. `Antialiasing::is_multisampled` (four or two samples) is what the performance forced may give up for FXAA (`ladder_on`, `lowered`), as before for four; SMAA, like FXAA, it keeps. The `antialias` system puts `Msaa::Sample2`, or `Smaa` on the world's cameras (and takes it off again), as it did FXAA. On the web neither is offered (WebGL2 and WebGPU promise only one or four samples, and Bevy's own anti-aliasing example is not built for the web, so SMAA is untried there). **Two samples only where the GPU has them** (found on lavapipe, section 1.5): wgpu allows a sample count past one and four only with `TEXTURE_ADAPTER_SPECIFIC_FORMAT_FEATURES` (which Bevy asks for whenever the adapter has it) and the adapter's format features allowing it, else the frame fails validation and Bevy quits. `TwoSamples` (`detail.rs`) is read at startup from `RenderAdapter` and `RenderDevice`: the feature, and two samples for `Rgba8UnormSrgb`, `Rgba8Unorm` (the formats Bevy draws the cameras' colour in without HDR) and `Depth32Float` (`CORE_3D_DEPTH_FORMAT`); the log says it ("Two samples a pixel (MSAA 2×): yes / not on this GPU"). Without it, Options does not offer MSAA 2× (`offered`), and `in_use` draws it as four should it come from a settings file (the HUD then says "MSAA 4x", not lowered); `in_use(chosen, msaa, two_samples)` is what the cameras, the HUD (`antialiasing_line`, lowered now only when the multisampling chosen was given up) and the performance log follow. On the web, FXAA for SMAA. The HUD says "MSAA 2x" and "SMAA"; the options "MSAA 2×" and "SMAA". SMAA's lookup textures come with Bevy's default features (`smaa_luts`): no new dependency.
- **The ground out of the shadows, in the benchmark** (`world/terrain.rs`, `bench.rs`): each mesh of the ground is marked `GroundPiece` (re-exported from `world`). `Scenario::ground_shadows` (true but in two scenarios); `cast_ground_shadows`, after `run_bench`, puts `NotShadowCaster` on the ground while the setting on (`Bench::setting_on`: the lap's first while its race is set up, then each in its turn) says so, and takes it off again; ground built again in such a turn is left out too. Bevy 0.19 follows `NotShadowCaster` coming and going (it is extracted on change and on removal) and it makes no new pipeline, so the turn's half second to settle is enough. It is not a setting of the game: it tells how much cutting the ground into pieces the cascades could leave out would save at most, before doing it.
- **The benchmark's scenarios** (22 a track, were 18): `1p-no-ground-shadows`, `1p-msaa-2x`, `1p-smaa` and `2p-no-ground-shadows`; the table's columns as before. Where the GPU has not got two samples, `1p-msaa-2x` is left out as the run begins (`Bench::leave_out`, the laps made again; else it would measure four samples again under its name), and the heading says "MSAA 2x left out: this GPU draws one or four samples a pixel". The screen's line names the smoothing "MSAA 4x", "MSAA 2x", "SMAA". The run's construction moved to `Bench::new` (for the tests). A whole run is about a fifth longer; `MOTORUST_BENCH_TRACKS` narrows it.
- **At most 8 steps a frame** (`main.rs`): `simulation_clocks` gives the fixed step (120 Hz, as before) and the game's time (`Time<Virtual>`) with a `max_delta` of 8 steps (`MAX_STEPS_PER_FRAME`), where Bevy's is a quarter of a second (30 steps). After a late frame (a hitch, a circuit put in the world) the race goes on by 8 steps at most instead of jumping on, and the frame after is not made longer by them; it keeps to real time down to 15 fps, slowing below that. The simulation costs 0.4–1 ms a frame now, so this is a guard, not a gain.
- Docs: `docs/gameplay.md` (Antialiasing, the performance HUD), `docs/architecture.md` (fixed-step physics, `main.rs`, `detail.rs`, `bench.rs`), `docs/building.md` (`make bench`).

### 1.4 Moving the Circuits to RON (Answered, Nothing Built)
- No effect on the frame rate: the circuit's data is read once and the terrain worked out from it either way; it neither helps nor blocks cutting the ground's meshes into pieces.
- Most of `src/circuits/*.rs` (8,344 lines) is data, but not all: each circuit has its `mountains` (`fn(Vec2) -> f32`), and `Coast::Function`, `OutlineWithLakes` and `Inland` take functions (the volcano's gullies, rivers' banks, lakes with an islet, coasts as sums of sines over `fbm`); constants are derived from others (`span(TUNNEL.from + 60.0, …)`) and built by `const fn` helpers (`knoll`, `fence`, `curve_sign`); the types take `&'static` slices and the circuits are `const`.
- Suggested, if the aim is to tune circuits without compiling: the layout and what is placed along it in RON (comments kept, unlike JSON), embedded with `include_str!` (the web and the single executable unchanged; read from disk in development to reload it), parsed once and leaked to `&'static` so that the code using it stays as it is; the functions kept in Rust and named from the file; a test that parses every circuit, so a mistake fails `cargo test` and not the game; one circuit first (Bahía GP, the simplest). `tools/circuit_layout.py` reads the Rust source with regular expressions today; it would need to read RON instead. The user's aim was asked.

### 1.5 Verification & Testing
- New tests: `every_smoothing_is_kept_and_only_the_samples_are_multisampled` (`settings.rs`), `the_ground_is_left_out_of_the_shadows_only_in_its_turn` and `two_samples_are_left_out_where_the_gpu_has_not_got_them` (`bench.rs`), `a_late_frame_catches_up_by_a_few_steps_at_most` (`main.rs`: 25 ms, three steps; 500 ms late, eight, the game's time on by eight steps; 25 ms, three again). Extended: `the_multisampling_given_up_is_fxaa_instead` (two samples as chosen with the GPU's, four without; SMAA, FXAA on the web; what Options offers) and the performance forced's `lowered` (two samples given up as four; SMAA kept), the antialiasing on the camera (`detail.rs`: four samples; two drawn as four until the GPU is known to have them, then two; SMAA, FXAA, none, and back), the HUD's line (four for two not marked lowered), the benchmark's scenarios (22 a track, one change from the reference but for two players; 17 and 5 a lap).
- A first run of the benchmark on lavapipe (Mesa's software Vulkan, under Xvfb, 1280 × 720, Bahía GP, `MOTORUST_BENCH_SECONDS=1`) quit in the warm-up at MSAA 2×: "Sample count 2 is not supported by format Rgba8UnormSrgb on this device. The WebGPU spec guarantees [1, 4] samples… With the TEXTURE_ADAPTER_SPECIFIC_FORMAT_FEATURES feature your device supports [1, 4]" (and the same for `Depth32Float`). Hence `TwoSamples`, above.
- The web build (`cargo check --target wasm32-unknown-unknown`) compiles, with the same five dead-code warnings in `perf_log.rs` as without these changes (what only the benchmark uses; clippy with `-D warnings` fails on them for wasm, and did before; the CI does not run it there).
- The benchmark again on lavapipe after `TwoSamples` (same setup): "Two samples a pixel (MSAA 2×): not on this GPU" in the log, "MSAA 2x left out: this GPU draws one or four samples a pixel" in the heading, the one-player lap warmed up and measured with its 16 settings (`1p-no-ground-shadows` and `1p-smaa` among them) and the two players' with 5 (`2p-no-ground-shadows`), no rendering error, `# finished`, exit 0. Its rates mean nothing there (0.4–1 fps). Both laps were given up part way (0.7 and 0.3 of 1.6 km): at about 1 fps the game's time now goes on 8 steps (67 ms) a frame, where it went on a quarter of a second, so the bike rides more slowly than the benchmark's real-time allowance for a lap; on any real GPU (over 15 fps) nothing changes.
- `cargo test`: 625 passed, 1 failed, 2 ignored while the lavapipe run took the four cores: `world::tests::circuits_are_prepared_in_the_menus_and_let_go_for_a_race`, which allows the circuits 120 s of wall-clock time to be prepared, passed on its own in 61 s (and in the first full run). So all 626 pass. `cargo fmt --check`: clean. `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings`: clean.
