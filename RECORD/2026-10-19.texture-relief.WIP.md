# Relief on the scenery's textures: normal maps and parallax, with a Graphics option

**Date:** 2026-10-19. **Branch:** `claude/sleepy-galileo-s35zhl`.

## 1. Normal maps and parallax, Options > Graphics > Relief, measured

### 1.1 User Request
- "How do you see applying a normal/parallax map to the textures?" Then: "Do performance measurements. An option in Graphics that lets the normal or the parallax be turned off will probably be needed."
- On the way: "Could the grass not look like a flat surface when the bikes go slowly? The same with the walls." And: "Aren't normal and parallax complementary?" (yes: the option's top level is both, the normal map lighting the bumps and parallax shifting them).

### 1.2 Implementation Details
- **The option** (`settings::Relief`, `Settings::relief`, saved; `RELIEFS`): "No" / "Off", "Normales" / "Normal maps" (the default), "Normales y parallax" / "Normals and parallax". A row of Graphics' own list under Antialiasing (`GraphicsItem::Relief`), reset with the section. Not lowered by the forced performance nor with two players: a normal map changes the pipeline its materials are drawn with.
- **The maps** (`src/world/relief.rs`): worked out at startup from each texture's own colours, as drawn (retouched), so they always fit it: `Heights::of` takes the lightness, a 3 × 3 box blur of it (a photo's grain is not relief) less its average over `broad` pixels each way (box blurs that repeat as the texture does), spread round ½ (`tanh` of it over twice its standard deviation). Lighter stands out, darker sinks in. The normal map (`normal_map`): two channels (`Rg8Unorm`, Bevy works out z), linear, from the heights' slopes for bumps `height` pixels high, x along u and y up the texture (against v), as Bevy's normal maps and parallax take them; with mipmaps (`generate_mipmaps` now does data of two and one channels too), which flatten the bumps away into the distance. The depth map, for the textures with parallax (`depth_map`): one channel (`R8Unorm`), 1 − height, without mipmaps (Bevy reads only its first level), its depth `height / width` of the texture's repetition (as Bevy takes it). `ReliefMaps::new` works them out each on a thread of its own (one thread on the web).
- **Which textures, and how** (`textures.rs`, `Bumps { broad, height, parallax }`): grass (8 px, 2.5 px, parallax), checkerboard earth (16, 3, parallax), rock and granite (24, 3), sand (12, 1.2), asphalt (8, 1), the stone wall (20, 5, parallax), roofs (24, 4, parallax), plaster (8, 1), the tunnels' panels (16, 2, parallax), planks (8, 1.5, parallax), strata (12, 2). The lawn in flower, painted as each circuit is put in the world, has the lawn's (`SharedRelief`, given by `build` in `world/mod.rs`).
- **The materials** (`relieve_materials`, in `Last`, after the materials made this frame are announced and before they go to the GPU): every `StandardMaterial` whose `base_color_texture` has relief gets it as the option says (`relieve`: the normal map; with parallax the depth map, its scale, 8 layers at most (`PARALLAX_LAYERS`, Bevy's own 16), occlusion mapping): those made this frame, and all of them when the option changes. No call site of the ~40 materials of the scenery changes.
- **The tangents** (`tangents`): a normal map needs them. Bevy's (MikkTSpace) takes 0.7 to 2.6 s a circuit; ours, each triangle's along u and v weighted by its area summed at its corners, the side of the bitangent as Bevy's gives it (tested against it on a plane, a sphere, a box and a cylinder), 12 to 60 ms. They are given whatever the option, so it can come on at any time: where a circuit is prepared, away from the main thread (`add_tangents` in `prepare`, for its ground and its roads' and structures' pieces drawn with relief); otherwise on the main thread in the frame a mesh is made (`give_tangents`). A mesh only for the GPU (`RenderAssetUsages::RENDER_WORLD`) is sent once and its vertices dropped: changing it with a tracked `get_mut` after that made it vanish ("cannot be extracted: The asset has already been extracted", the asphalt and the rock gone from the menu); now it is changed untracked before it goes, and one gone already is left without (drawn as before).
- **The benchmark** (`bench.rs`): `relief` in each scenario, `Relief::Normal` as the game starts; new scenarios `1p-relief-off`, `1p-parallax`, `2p-relief-off`; the setting on shown under the frame rate. The performance log writes `relief=` too.
- Docs: `docs/gameplay.md` (the option), `docs/architecture.md` (`relief.rs`, `textures.rs`).

### 1.3 Verification & Testing
- New tests: `an_even_texture_is_level`, `light_stands_out_and_dark_sinks_in`, `heights_repeat_as_the_texture_does`, `blurring_keeps_the_average_and_spreads_a_spike`, `the_maps_are_data_with_their_mipmaps_or_without`, `materials_take_the_relief_chosen`, `tangents_run_along_u_with_the_bitangent_up_the_texture`, `tangents_agree_with_bevys_own`, `only_meshes_of_triangles_with_texture_coordinates_have_tangents`, `the_relief_starts_with_normal_maps_and_is_kept` (settings saved before it come back with normal maps), `the_relief_is_on_graphics_own_list_under_the_antialiasing_and_resets_with_it`; the bench's tests count the new scenarios.
- `cargo test --release`: 595 passed, 0 failed, 2 ignored. `cargo fmt --check`: clean. `cargo clippy --release --all-targets -- -D warnings`: clean.
- **CPU, measured here** (a temporary timing test, not committed; release, 4 cores): the maps of each texture 1 to 50 ms on one thread (512 × 512: 23 to 27 ms; the stone wall, 1536 × 384: 50 ms; 186 ms in all), all of them on threads at startup; the tangents of what each circuit draws with relief (0.26 to 1.4 million vertices): ours 12 to 60 ms, Bevy's MikkTSpace 0.66 to 2.6 s.
- **GPU: not measurable here.** The container has no GPU; the game runs on Mesa's llvmpipe under Xvfb. A temporary probe (not committed: the bike on Bahía's grid, the camera still, the relief taking turns Off, Normal, Parallax, rotating, frame times after settling) gave 1.2 fps whatever the relief (1280 × 720: Off 822 ms a frame, Normal 897, Parallax 845, 30 frames each; again with longer turns: 847, 820, 821 ms, 78 frames each), all within the noise: the frame is bound by geometry on the CPU there, not by pixels. The real cost is to be measured with `make bench` on the Macs (M1 Pro, M4 Pro), comparing `1p` with `1p-relief-off` and `1p-parallax`, and `2p` with `2p-relief-off`.

## 2. The grass's repetition broken up: a wide lawn, or stochastic tiling (Options > Graphics > Grass tiling)

**Date:** 2026-10-20. **Branch:** `claude/sleepy-galileo-s35zhl`.

### 2.1 User Request
- A suggestion passed on: "Break the tiling. On grass the repetition is what gives it away most. Multiply by a low-frequency noise texture (macro variation), add a detail texture at another scale and, if you want more, an ExtendedMaterial with stochastic tiling." Weighed up (the ground's colours already vary over the land by vertex colour, `terrain::lawn_tint`: the macro variation is there; the repetition left is the lawn's own clumps every 11 m), two ways offered: A, a wide lawn put together at startup; B, stochastic tiling in a shader.
- "About the tiling, implement A and B. We add to Graphics Options an option with the three possibilities: none, A or B. A selected by default."

### 2.2 Implementation Details
- **The option** (`settings::GrassTiling`, `Settings::grass_tiling`, saved; `GRASS_TILINGS`): "Ninguna" / "None", "Textura amplia" / "Wide texture" (the default), "Estocástica" / "Stochastic". A row of Graphics' own list under the relief's (`GraphicsItem::GrassTiling`, "Repetición de la hierba" / "Grass tiling"), reset with the section.
- **Both ways** (`src/world/lawn.rs`): the ground cut into triangles, each corner reading the lawn shifted by a random amount of its own (Jarzynski and Olano's PCG 2D hash), the three pieces of each triangle blended round the lawn's average colour, their differences from it divided by the square root of the sum of their weights squared, so the blend keeps the lawn's contrast instead of greying it (after Heitz and Neyret, "High-Performance By-Example Noise using a Histogram-Preserving Blending Operator", 2018); the weights raised to the 4th power (`SHARP`) and summed to 1 again, so most of the ground is one piece or another and only their edges cross-fade.
- **A, the wide lawn** (`widen`, `WIDE`, `PATCH`): 4 times the lawn each way (2048 px, 44 m), from squares of 128 px (2.75 m), each split along one diagonal or the other as its own hash says, the corners' shifts repeating with it (so it repeats without seams); blended in 8-bit sRGB, in four bands of rows on threads (one on the web); with its mipmaps. Made at startup on a thread of its own while the other textures are (`WorldTextures::from_world`), and given its relief as the lawn's (`ReliefMaps`, `GRASS_BUMPS`). Every material drawn with the lawn (the ground, the banks, the hedges) takes it (`tile`, `tile_materials` in `Last`, before the relief is given out): its texture the wide lawn, stretched over 4 times as much (`uv_transform` scaled by ¼, and back by 4), with the wide lawn's relief; given the lawn back for the other two.
- **B, stochastic tiling** (`LawnMaterial`, `ExtendedMaterial<StandardMaterial, StochasticLawn>`, `src/world/lawn.wgsl`): the pieces picked as each pixel is drawn, over a grid of equilateral triangles (a skewed grid) a third of the lawn across (`STOCHASTIC_CELLS`), the three readings with the lawn's own derivatives (`textureSampleGrad`), so their mipmaps and anisotropic filtering are the lawn's; over the material's colour and the ground's tint. Its normal map read and blended the same way, put into the material's normal (as Bevy's mikktspace normal mapping) if the relief has one; no parallax (three times its readings). The material is the lawn's own (`StandardMaterial` without its texture and maps, all else as it was), so the light, the fog and the shadows are Bevy's. Every thing drawn with the lawn has its material swapped for one of these (`draw_stochastic`, after its mesh has its tangents; one for each material of the lawn, so what drew together still does), its own kept in `Lawn` and given back when the tiling changes.
- The benchmark (`bench.rs`): `grass_tiling` in each scenario (`Wide` as the game starts), new scenarios `1p-grass-plain` and `1p-grass-stochastic`, the tiling under the frame rate; the performance log writes `grass_tiling=`.
- Docs: `docs/gameplay.md` (the option), `docs/architecture.md` (`lawn.rs`, `lawn.wgsl`).

### 2.3 Verification & Testing
- New tests: `the_triangles_weights_add_up_and_meet_at_the_squares_edges`, `sharpened_weights_add_up_and_favour_the_heaviest`, `the_wide_lawn_repeats_without_seams`, `the_wide_lawn_is_the_lawn_in_pieces_with_its_colours` (its mean colour within 3 of the lawn's in 8 bits, its stripes as light and as dark, most of it not the lawn over again), `the_lawn_s_materials_take_the_wide_lawn_and_give_it_back`, `the_grass_starts_with_the_wide_texture_and_is_kept`, `the_grass_tiling_is_under_the_relief_and_resets_with_graphics`; the bench's tests count the new scenarios.

- Screenshots (temporary harness under Xvfb, not committed): Fellowship Road's grid and Loop Island at 300 m with each tiling: the three drawn alike close up (no shader error, the same greens), the stochastic one compiled and lit as the rest.

## 3. Relief on the road as an option; the relief dropped at speed; the cartoon water's name

**Date:** 2026-10-20. **Branch:** `claude/sleepy-galileo-s35zhl`.

### 3.1 User Request
- "Add to Options > Graphics the option of applying relief to the road, off by default."
- "Add the option in Graphics to turn the relief off with speed, on by default. This option would take away the relief effect with speed, except for the road if the road option were on."
- "In Options > Graphics, change the name of the water option 'Wind Waker' to 'Estilo Cartoon'. Only the name in the options."

### 3.2 Implementation Details
- **Relief on the road** (`Settings::road_relief`, off; `GraphicsItem::RoadRelief`, "Relieve en la carretera" / "Relief on the road", a check under Relief): the materials drawn with the asphalt (`relief::is_road`: the roads, and Loop Island's loops) have the relief chosen only with it on; otherwise none, as before relief was added (no normal map: the pipeline they were drawn with).
- **No relief at high speed** (`Settings::flat_at_speed`, on; `GraphicsItem::FlatAtSpeed`, "Sin relieve a alta velocidad" / "No relief at high speed", a check under the road's): while all the players (`Player`, `LocalPlayer`) go faster than 80 km/h (`relief::FAST`), the relief of everything but the road (when it has it) is dropped, and given back once one of them goes slower than 60 km/h (`SLOW`; apart, so it does not come and go at one speed); without players (the menus), never (`relief::fast`). What each material shows (`relief::Look`: `Off`, `Flat`, `Normal`, `Parallax`) comes from `ReliefShown` (the relief chosen, the road's, and whether the rest is flattened), worked out each frame (`show_relief`, in `Last` before the materials are given their relief); all the materials with relief are given it again when it changes. Dropped, a material's normal map is swapped for a flat one of one pixel (`ReliefMap::flat`, `flat_normal_map`) and its depth map taken away, rather than its normal map taken away: a normal map changes the pipeline (Bevy's `StandardMaterialKey::NORMAL_MAP`) and what it draws would vanish for a moment as it is made, in the middle of a race; a depth map is only a flag. The stochastic lawn drops its normal map with the rest (`LawnLook::normals`).
- **The cartoon water's name** (`options.rs`): the value of Graphics' Water row "Estilo Cartoon" / "Cartoon style" (was "Wind Waker" in both); the code and its comments still call it after *The Wind Waker*.
- The benchmark: `road_relief` and `flat_at_speed` in each scenario (off and on, as the game starts); new scenarios `1p-relief-at-speed` (relief kept at speed) and `1p-road-relief`; `1p-parallax` keeps its relief at speed (what it measures); shown under the frame rate when not as at first ("(road too)", "at speed too"). The performance log writes `road_relief=` and `flat_at_speed=`.
- Docs: `docs/gameplay.md` (the options, the water's name), `docs/architecture.md` (`relief.rs`, `sea.rs`).
