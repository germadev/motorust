# Relief on the scenery's textures: normal maps and parallax, with a Graphics option

**Date:** 2026-10-19. **Branch:** `claude/sleepy-galileo-s35zhl`.

## 1. Normal maps and parallax, Options > Graphics > Relief, measured

### 1.1 User Request
- "How do you see applying a normal/parallax map to the textures?" Then: "Do performance measurements. An option in Graphics that lets the normal or the parallax be turned off will probably be needed."
- On the way: "Could the grass not look like a flat surface when the bikes go slowly? The same with the walls." And: "Aren't normal and parallax complementary?" (yes: the option's top level is both, the normal map lighting the bumps and parallax shifting them).

### 1.2 Implementation Details
- **The option** (`settings::Relief`, `Settings::relief`, saved; `RELIEFS`): "No" / "Off", "Normales" / "Normal maps" (the default), "Normales y parallax" / "Normals and parallax". A row of Graphics' own list under Antialiasing (`GraphicsItem::Relief`), reset with the section. Not lowered by the forced performance nor with two players: a normal map changes the pipeline its materials are drawn with.
- **The maps** (`src/world/relief.rs`): worked out at startup from each texture's own colours, as drawn (retouched), so they always fit it: `Heights::of` takes the lightness, a 3 × 3 box blur of it (a photo's grain is not relief) less its average over `broad` pixels each way (box blurs that repeat as the texture does), spread round ½ (`tanh` of it over twice its standard deviation). Lighter stands out, darker sinks in. The normal map (`normal_map`): two channels (`Rg8Unorm`, Bevy works out z), linear, from the heights' slopes for bumps `height` pixels high, x along u and y up the texture (against v), as Bevy's normal maps and parallax take them; with mipmaps (`generate_mipmaps` now does data of two and one channels too), which flatten the bumps away into the distance. The depth map, for the textures with parallax (`depth_map`): one channel (`R8Unorm`), 1 − height, without mipmaps (Bevy reads only its first level), its depth `height / width` of the texture's repetition (as Bevy takes it). `ReliefMaps::new` works them out each on a thread of its own (one thread on the web).
- **Which textures, and how** (`textures.rs`, `Bumps { broad, height, parallax }`): grass (8 px, 2.5 px, parallax), checkerboard earth (16, 3, parallax), rock and granite (24, 3), sand (12, 1.2), asphalt (8, 1), the stone wall (20, 5, parallax), roofs (24, 4, parallax), plaster (8, 1), the tunnels' panels (16, 2, parallax), planks (8, 1.5, parallax), strata (12, 2). The lawn in flower, painted as each circuit is put in the world, has the lawn's (`SharedRelief`, given by `build` in `world/mod.rs`).
- **The materials** (`relieve_materials`, in `Last`, after the materials made this frame are announced and before they go to the GPU): every `StandardMaterial` whose `base_color_texture` has relief gets it as the option says (`relieve`: the normal map; with parallax the depth map, its scale, 8 layers at most (`PARALLAX_LAYERS`, Bevy's own 16), occlusion mapping): those made this frame, and all of them when the option changes. No call site of the ~40 materials of the scenery changes.
- **The tangents** (`tangents`): a normal map needs them. Bevy's (MikkTSpace) takes 0.7 to 2.6 s a circuit; ours, each triangle's along u and v weighted by its area summed at its corners, the side of the bitangent as Bevy's gives it (tested against it on a plane, a sphere, a box and a cylinder), 12 to 60 ms. They are given whatever the option, so it can come on at any time: where a circuit is prepared, away from the main thread (`add_tangents` in `prepare`, for its ground and its roads' and structures' pieces drawn with relief); otherwise on the main thread in the frame a mesh is made (`give_tangents`). A mesh only for the GPU (`RenderAssetUsages::RENDER_WORLD`) is sent once and its vertices dropped: changing it with a tracked `get_mut` after that made it vanish ("cannot be extracted: The asset has already been extracted", the asphalt and the rock gone from the menu); now it is changed untracked before it goes, and one gone already is left without (drawn as before).
- **The benchmark** (`bench.rs`): `relief` in each scenario, `Relief::Normal` as the game starts; new scenarios `1p-relief-off`, `1p-parallax`, `2p-relief-off`; the setting on shown under the frame rate. The performance log writes `relief=` too.
- Docs: `docs/gameplay.md` (the option), `docs/architecture.md` (`relief.rs`, `textures.rs`).

### 1.3 Verification & Testing
- New tests: `an_even_texture_is_level`, `light_stands_out_and_dark_sinks_in`, `heights_repeat_as_the_texture_does`, `blurring_keeps_the_average_and_spreads_a_spike`, `the_maps_are_data_with_their_mipmaps_or_without`, `materials_take_the_relief_chosen`, `tangents_run_along_u_with_the_bitangent_up_the_texture`, `tangents_agree_with_bevys_own`, `only_meshes_of_triangles_with_texture_coordinates_have_tangents`, `the_relief_starts_with_normal_maps_and_is_kept` (settings saved before it come back with normal maps), `the_relief_is_on_graphics_own_list_under_the_antialiasing_and_resets_with_it`; the bench's tests count the new scenarios.
- `cargo test --release`: 595 passed, 0 failed, 2 ignored. `cargo fmt --check`: clean. `cargo clippy --release --all-targets -- -D warnings`: clean.
- **CPU, measured here** (a temporary timing test, not committed; release, 4 cores): the maps of each texture 1 to 50 ms on one thread (512 × 512: 23 to 27 ms; the stone wall, 1536 × 384: 50 ms; 186 ms in all), all of them on threads at startup; the tangents of what each circuit draws with relief (0.26 to 1.4 million vertices): ours 12 to 60 ms, Bevy's MikkTSpace 0.66 to 2.6 s.
- **GPU: not measurable here.** The container has no GPU; the game runs on Mesa's llvmpipe under Xvfb. A temporary probe (not committed: the bike on Bahía's grid, the camera still, the relief taking turns Off, Normal, Parallax, rotating, frame times after settling) gave 1.2 fps whatever the relief (1280 × 720: Off 822 ms a frame, Normal 897, Parallax 845, 30 frames each; again with longer turns: 847, 820, 821 ms, 78 frames each), all within the noise: the frame is bound by geometry on the CPU there, not by pixels. The real cost is to be measured with `make bench` on the Macs (M1 Pro, M4 Pro), comparing `1p` with `1p-relief-off` and `1p-parallax`, and `2p` with `2p-relief-off`.
- Screenshots (temporary harness under Xvfb, not committed): the menu over Bahía's rock cliffs, their cracks lit and shaded, the stone wall bumpy; Bahía's grid with each relief (the stone walls' stones shaded with normal maps, shifting with parallax).
