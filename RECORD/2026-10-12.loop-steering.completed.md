# Steering Round the Loop

- **Date:** 2026-10-12
- **Topic:** Let the bikes steer while they ride round a vertical loop, instead of running on rails.
- **Branch:** `claude/affectionate-volta-sylwuc`

## User Request

> "Por qué no se puede girar en el looping?"

After the explanation (on the loop the bike ran on rails: `loop_ride::enter` and `loop_ride::ride` zeroed the steering, and `Ride::lateral` never changed), the user asked to implement it:

> "Si, impleméntalo"

## Implementation Details

### Riding the loop with a yaw (`src/bike/loop_ride.rs`)

- `Ride` gains `yaw`: which way the bike points on the running surface (rad, in [-π, π), 0 straight round, positive to the left like the heading). On the loop `BikeState::heading` stays the loop's; off it the yaw goes back into the heading.
- `enter`: the bike keeps the way it pointed on the road as its yaw (`wrap_angle(heading - loop.heading)`), and its heading becomes the loop's; it goes in when facing into the mouth (`cos(yaw) > 0.5`, as before). The steering is no longer reset.
- `ride`: `step` now runs with the player's (or rival's) steering. The change of heading it computes is the turn on the running surface, scaled by the grip: how hard the surface presses on the bike over gravity, up to 1 (as on a road), so slow near the top the bike hardly turns. The bike moves round the loop by `travel·cos(yaw)/arc` and across it by `travel·sin(yaw)`, pointing half way through the turn. Only the speed round the loop counts for how hard the surface presses (`pressed`: `(v·cos yaw)²/R + g·cos θ`) and for the suspension (`crest = -cos²(yaw)/R`), and gravity slows the bike by `g·sin θ·cos(yaw)`.
- The running surface carries the bike sideways as it goes round (the loop's `shift`), so riding straight on still follows it out beside the mouth: the yaw is measured from the way the running surface goes, the same simplification the rails had (the drift is not counted in the bike's orientation, so the road in and out join the loop with no kink).
- `hit_walls`: the walls along the edges of the running surface (meshes at `LOOP_WALL` from the middle) keep the bike on it. `RIDE_HALF_WIDTH` is now `LOOP_WALL - BIKE_HALF_WIDTH` (6.05 m). As `bike::hit_walls` does on the road, a bike that goes into one stays against it, loses `IMPACT_SPEED_LOSS·impact²` of its speed (impact: the sine of the angle into the wall) and turns `WALL_ALIGN` of the way along it. Nobody falls against these walls: a fallen bike sliding on the loop is not simulated. The new `Outcome::HitWall(into)` reports the speed into the wall; `Outcome::riding()` covers it and `Riding`.
- `leave`: the bike comes out pointing the way it was (`heading += yaw`). Falling off, the heading takes the yaw with its sign flipped upside down (`yaw·sign(cos θ)`): seen from above, upside down, a bike turned to its left points to the right of the way round, which is where `crash::fall_off` sends it and makes its rider face. The fall velocity includes the part across the loop.
- `place`: the vertical speed counts only the speed round the loop.

### Drawing it (`src/bike/mod.rs`)

- `BikeState::orientation`: `Y(heading)·X(pitch)·Y(yaw)`, the last turn about the bike's own up, which is down at the top (a turn about the world's vertical would turn the bike the wrong way upside down).
- `interpolate_bikes` slerps between the two orientations instead of lerping heading and pitch apart; out of a loop the pitch going from a whole turn to zero needs no special case any more.
- `simulate_bikes`: hitting a loop's wall is felt in the controller like a road wall (`CollisionImpact::record`, never a fall).

### The chase camera (`src/camera.rs`)

- On a loop the camera's yaw follows `heading + yaw` (the heading the bike will have off the loop), and the new `loop_tilt` puts the camera in the bike's frame turned back by how far the camera still lags behind, about the bike's own up. Caught up, the camera is right behind the bike; out of the loop, the tilt is the identity, so it carries on smoothly from the road behaviour.

### Rivals (`src/bike/ai.rs`)

- With the steering live round the loop, rivals must steer: `loop_steer` points them gently towards their line (at most 0.15 rad, getting there in about a second) and straight on again, with a turn that settles in about 0.3 s, through the same steering spring as on the road. Their line is clamped to the approach road's asphalt less `EDGE_MARGIN`.

## Verification & Testing

- New tests in `src/bike/loop_ride.rs` (5 → 11):
  - `steering_takes_the_bike_across_the_running_surface`: steering 0.3 either way at 35 m/s for 0.4 s turns the bike (> 0.1 rad), takes it across (> 0.5 m) and leans it into the turn; let go, it keeps going that way without turning back.
  - `the_bike_turns_about_its_own_up_on_the_running_surface`: `BikeState::orientation` keeps the loop's up as the bike's up and points along `tangent·cos(yaw) + left·sin(yaw)`, at the bottom, the side, the top and on the way down.
  - `the_walls_along_the_edges_keep_the_bike_on_the_running_surface`: turned 0.05 and 0.4 rad into a wall, the bike never goes past it, hits it at `v·sin(yaw)`, ends against it pointing along it, and keeps 99–100 % and 80–95 % of the speed of a bike riding straight on.
  - `turned_on_the_loop_the_bike_comes_out_pointing_that_way`: turned 0.04 rad from 3 m right of the middle, it crosses `length·tan(yaw)` and comes out of the way out heading `loop.heading + yaw`.
  - `the_less_the_surface_presses_the_less_the_bike_turns`: at the top, pressed a fifth as hard as on the road, the bike turns a fifth as much as at the bottom.
  - `turned_upside_down_the_bike_falls_off_the_way_it_was_going`: falling off at the top turned 0.3 rad, its velocity, the way it pointed and `forward(heading + π)` (where the rider faces) agree.
  - `a_bike_at_the_mouth_goes_onto_the_loop` extended: a bike turned 0.3 rad goes in with that yaw and the loop's heading; turned 1.2 rad it does not go in.
- New test in `src/bike/ai.rs`: `round_a_loop_a_rival_gets_to_its_line_clear_of_the_walls` (a rival in at 3 m left, turned 0.1 rad away from its −2 m line, stays within 4.5 m of the middle and comes out within 0.3 m of its line, straight on). The existing loop tests for rivals still pass.
- New test in `src/camera.rs`: `round_a_loop_the_camera_follows_the_bike_turning_on_the_running_surface` (caught up, the camera's frame is the bike's; lagging, it keeps the bike's up; out of the loop no tilt is left). A first version compared quaternions with `angle_between`, whose `acos` near 1 turns `f32` rounding into 4·10⁻⁴ rad at a pitch of π/2; it now compares the rotated axes.
- `Outcome::riding` is only used by the tests, so it is `#[cfg(test)]` (clippy's dead code).
- `cargo test`: **229 passed**, 0 failed (221 before; the docs said 216, which was out of date, and now say 229).
- `cargo fmt --check`: clean. `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings`: clean.
- Not run in a window (no display in the container): the feel of the steering and the camera round the loop is checked by the tests above only.
