# A Speedometer Dial in the Race HUD

**Date:** 2026-10-13
**Branch:** `claude/loving-maxwell-vr7uub`

## 1. User Request
- "Añade este velocímetro al HUD de la carrera, abajo a la derecha" (add this speedometer, an attached SVG design `velocimetro_v3.svg`, to the race HUD, bottom right).
- While it was being built: "El degradado del velocímetro debería ir de transparente al negro transparente" (the dial's gradient should go from transparent to translucent black), "El disco central y el anillo también deberían ser translúcidos" (the central disc and the rim should be translucent too) and "Oscurece un poco los negros translúcidos" (make the translucent blacks a little darker).

## 2. Implementation Details
- **The design** (380-unit view box, dial centred on 190, 190): a disc of radius 150 with a thin grey edge, a rim 10 wide at radius 145 in 50 % black, a face of radius 140 shaded with a radial gradient from clear to 40 % black, a red arc 6 wide along the face from 300 to 360 km/h, the speeds from 0 to 360 every 30 at radius 118 round three quarters of the dial (14 units tall up to 180, one unit more each step after, red from 300), a blue triangular pointer from radius 52 to 98, and a 50 % black disc of radius 52 with the speed and "km/h". As asked, the design's opaque cream disc is left out: the face is see-through, and the rim and the central disc are translucent black over the race; and the blacks are a little darker than in the design: 60 % for the rim and the central disc, and the face's gradient out to 50 %.
- **`src/speedometer.rs`** (new, `SpeedometerPlugin`, added by `HudPlugin`):
  - The dial is drawn on the CPU, once per size, at the exact size it takes on the screen: the shapes as SVG path data rasterized with zeno (strokes in the design's units, scaled with it), the figures with the UI font (Russo One), each layer laid over the last in sRGB as a browser draws an SVG. The pointer is a second image the size of the dial, pointing up, turned every frame with the node's `UiTransform` (`pointer_angle`: −135° at 0 km/h to +135° at 360, clamped). The speed in figures and "km/h" are Bevy text nodes in the middle.
  - `draw_dials` (in `PostUpdate`, after the UI layout) gives each dial and pointer the images for its laid-out size (physical px), drawing a new size when it first appears (a resized window) and letting go of sizes no longer shown. The images are shared by both players' dials and kept between races. Per frame, only the pointer's rotation and the figures change, and only when the speed does.
- **`src/hud.rs`**: the speed text ("123 km/h") is replaced by the dial: 220 px wide, bottom right, alone; 160 px wide at the bottom right of each half with two players.
- **`src/vector_art.rs`**: the lettering options (slant, weight, tracking) are a `Lettering` value, so the same code draws the menu's slanted heavy letters and, with `draw_plain_text`, the speedometer's plain figures.
- **Docs**: `docs/gameplay.md` (race HUD table), `docs/architecture.md` (code map and the test table, brought up to date: 259 tests).

## 3. Verification & Testing
- New tests: `the_scale_runs_round_three_quarters_of_the_dial`, `the_dial_is_drawn_as_in_the_design` (clear outside, black face more opaque towards the edge, translucent black rim, red arc, translucent central disc), `the_speeds_are_marked_round_the_dial` (white figures up to 270, red from 300), `the_pointer_points_up_from_the_middle`, `laying_colour_over_a_clear_pixel_gives_that_colour`.
- `cargo test`: 259 passed. `cargo fmt --check` clean, `cargo clippy --all-targets` no warnings, `cargo check --target wasm32-unknown-unknown` builds.
- Compared a render of the dial with the SVG rendered in Chromium: same layout and colours (the figures are in the game's font instead of Helvetica). Ran the game under Xvfb with Mesa lavapipe: one player, the dial bottom right with the pointer and the figures following the speed (0, 50, 62 km/h); two players, a dial at the bottom right of each half, each with its own player's speed; after the change to a see-through face, the race shows through the dial and the figures stay readable over the road.
