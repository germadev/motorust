# Moto Racer remake in Rust and Bevy

- **Status:** in progress (WIP).
- **Dates:** 8-10 October 2026.
- **Branch:** `claude/optimistic-wozniak-pw99ce`.
- **PRs:**
  - [germadev/motorust#1](https://github.com/germadev/motorust/pull/1): the first version.
  - [germadev/motorust#2](https://github.com/germadev/motorust/pull/2): everything else up to the exhausts.
  - Both were *squash*-merged into `main`. The branch was then recreated from `main`.

## Goal

Make an arcade motorcycle racing game inspired by **Moto Racer 1**. What we like most about MR1:

- the design;
- the sounds and the music;
- the gameplay, the handling and the sense of speed.

The technical priority is performance.

## Initial decisions

| Topic | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Programming language | Rust (over Go and C++) | C++ performance without a garbage collector and with memory safety. Mature game ecosystem. |
| Engine | Bevy 0.19 | An ECS that spreads systems across cores effortlessly, and everything in Rust. |
| Physics | Fixed 120 Hz step with interpolated rendering | Stable behaviour, independent of the FPS. |
| Logic | Pure functions with tests (`bike::step`, `track`, `ai::decide`, `pilot::pose`…) | Testable without the ECS: AI, replays or networking in the future. |
| Language | Code, comments and commits in Spanish; README, docs/ and this RECORD in English | The user writes in Spanish; the public documentation is in English (since block 36). |
| Assets | CC0 textures from Poly Haven, the Russo One font (OFL) and Meshy models from the repo's releases | Free licences. Credits in `assets/CREDITS.md`. |
| Brands | No real brands: no logos on the bikes and fictional bike names | Colours and styles are imitated, not brands (see block 36). |

### 14. Corners that don't cost speed

Request: "In corners it seems to lose a bit of speed, it should be the other way round".

Diagnosis with a temporary probe:
- At 60 m/s with steering at 0.5, the bike gained 1 m/s in 2 s. On the straight it gained 9.
- With steering at 0.7 it lost 6.5 m/s.
- There were two causes: the cornering brake (`CORNER_SCRUB`) acted on any steering input, and so did the engine cut (`CORNER_POWER_CUT`).

Done:
- **Normal corners** (steering below 0.7): no braking, and the engine pushes up to 40% more (`CORNER_DRIVE`). The bike shoots out of corners.
- **When pushing to the limit** (from 0.7 to full lock):
  - the extra push fades out;
  - the bike grips up to 60% more (`CORNER_GRIP`);
  - the engine pushes less;
  - it brakes at up to 25 m/s² to tighten the corner.
- **Lateral grip:** from 55 to 65 m/s².
  - To compensate, the keyboard's moderate steering at high speed drops from 0.3 to 0.25, and line changes stay just as smooth.
- **Jumps:** with more speed, the AI reached the crest at 73 m/s and flew 1.55 m for 0.75 s.
  - Airborne gravity goes from 1.8 g to 2 g on the way up and from 3.2 g to 4 g on the way down.
  - With that the jump stays at 1 m and 0.65 s.
- **Result on the flat-out lap:** average speed rises from 56 to 66 m/s and it still doesn't touch the walls, with margin to spare.
- **Tests:**
  - new, `moderate_corners_flat_out_gain_speed`: with steering at 0.3, 0.5 or 0.65 the bike gains more speed than on the straight;
  - the full-lock test still checks that speed is lost to tighten the corner.

### 15. Exhausts with an outlet

Request: "The exhaust pipes have no hole, take a look at real photos to see what it would look like".

Reference photos from Wikimedia Commons: the tail sections of four real sport bikes, one of them an endurance racer with a carbon-fibre aftermarket silencer.
- The outlet is an almost black hollow with the shape of the silencer: round, oval or trapezoidal.
- It takes up a little more than half of the end cap.
- It is surrounded by the thin metal lip of the inner pipe, which sticks out slightly.

What there was: the Meshy silencers ended in a solid cap, domed on the Shogun TZ.

Done:
- **Detection in `tools/import_models.py`.** It recognises the silencers: chassis parts elongated towards the rear, round (not flat), with a 5 to 14 cm radius and off to one side. They must have an end cap and be far from the rear axle. This rules out the Stiletto V4's swingarm and the Azura RZ's subframe tubes.
- **Outlets detected:** 8 in total.

  | Bike | Outlets | Shape |
  |---|---|---|
  | Shogun TZ | 2 | round |
  | Kraken RS | 1 | round |
  | Falcon KRZ | 1 | round |
  | Azura RZ | 2 | trapezoids |
  | Legend 88 | 1 | pentagon |
  | Stiletto V4 | 1 | under the engine |

- **First attempt:** a flat tube on the end cap. It stuck out 3 cm past the edge of the dome and looked like a loose pipe.
- **Final version:** the outlet is fitted into the end cap.
  - The rim, inner wall and bottom follow the surface of the cap, 2 mm above it.
  - The 5 mm lip sticks out 1.2 cm around the whole outline.
  - Cutting the mesh would have been fragile: the caps are irregular fans.
- **New `exhaust` part in `bike.bin`.** The other four parts come out byte-for-byte identical.
- **Dedicated in-game material:**
  - a one-row texture with a gradient from metal to soot to black, along the u coordinate;
  - no reflections, because with the ambient light the hollow looked grey instead of deep.
- **Tests:** the outlets are at the rear, off to one side and above the ground; the gradient goes from light metal to black.
- **Verification:** close-up screenshots of the six bikes.

### 19. Controller on macOS (`5c6625c`)

User's details: GameSir T3 Lite on macOS, on an M4 Pro Mac. Sometimes it isn't detected until it is reconnected.

Reading the gilrs-core 0.6.8 code (macOS):
- Already-connected controllers are searched for on another thread, with IOHIDManager, after startup.
- If opening a device fails at that moment, or the indices get out of step between its device list and its controller list, the controller is lost until it is reconnected.
- It is already the latest published version.

Done:
- **Custom `src/gamepads.rs` module** instead of Bevy's (`GilrsPlugin` disabled). It does the same thing and, in addition, while there is no controller, every 2.5 s (wall-clock time) it creates a new gilrs that searches for them again, as if they were reconnected.
- **Limit of 8 searches:** on macOS each one leaves a thread behind.
- **Start screen:** it says "Buscando mando..." ("Searching for controller...") and, if in the end there is none, "Sin mando: si tienes uno, reconéctalo" ("No controller: if you have one, reconnect it").
- **Verified here:** the game starts, repeats the search without errors and the warnings change on time.
- **Not verified:** that it finds the controller on a Mac. There is no way to simulate one here, because `/dev/uinput` doesn't exist.

### 23. Leaning over doesn't cost speed, not with the keyboard either

Request: "When turning/leaning over it loses speed, it should be the other way round".

Diagnosis with a temporary probe:
- The speed loss when pushing to the limit started at 70% steering.
- The keyboard, at full speed, reaches 80%. At 78 m/s, holding it lost 6.8 m/s in 2 s.
- The loss was first disabled completely. Then the flat-out lap using only steering brushed the walls, even if the push-to-the-limit grip was tripled or the test driver looked further ahead. At an average of about 77 m/s, corners with a 40 m radius can't be taken without losing speed.

Done:
- **Two separate zones:**
  - From 70% steering, the bike grips more and more. At full lock it grips 60% more at low speed and up to 140% more at high speed, in proportion to speed between 25 and 60 m/s. At low speed it stays the same as before, because there the turning radius is what limits it.
  - Speed is lost only from 85% (up to 35 m/s² at speed) and the engine pushes less. That is reserved for the tightest corners with steering almost at full lock.
- **Below 85%:** the bike pushes up to 40% more than on the straight. The keyboard at full speed (80%) gains speed instead of losing it.
- **Flat-out lap using only steering:** it still doesn't touch the walls, with 1.1 m of margin and 134 of the 359 allowed steps on the grass. It also passes with slight variations of each parameter.
- **Tests:** the moderate-corners test also checks 80%.

### 30. Sound without stutter and a textured tunnel ceiling

Requests:
- "Fix the sounds, they sound choppy, look for more realistic sounds."
- "The tunnel ceiling seems to have no texture."

Causes of the choppy sound (both in the game, not in the synthesizer):
- There were 6 engine voices for 8 bikes, reassigned every frame to the 6 nearest. When two bikes swapped places in distance (on the grid, all the time), a voice jumped from one engine to another: different revs and a different volume, in jerks.
- The Doppler used the camera velocity measured from one frame to the next, which jitters. That jitter shifted the pitch of all the engines, including the player's.

Done:
- **Voices:** 8, one per bike, always the same one (in creation order).
- **Doppler:** the camera velocity is smoothed (0.25 s); the player's engine has no Doppler, and the rivals' stays between 0.8 and 1.25.
- **Parameters:** smoothed over 50 ms instead of 20, longer than a slow frame.
- **Lighter audio thread:**
  - the per-sample constants are computed once;
  - the mix no longer clones the `Arc` on every sample;
  - the one-shot sound queue is checked every 256 samples, not on every one.
- **More realistic sounds:** Freesound and Pixabay are blocked by the environment's network policy. OpenGameArt only has an engine made with the mouth (CC0) and some car loops with a disputed licence. Wikimedia Commons responds, but rate-limits queries: search pending.
- **Tunnel:**
  - new procedural texture of concrete panels with joints, on the ceiling and walls;
  - ribs from side to side under the ceiling every 8 m;
  - the walkway starts at the edge of the tarmac.

### 31. DualSense on macOS with GameController

Request: "My DualSense doesn't work on macOS."

Diagnosis (without a Mac at hand):
- gilrs reads controllers on macOS with IOKit (HID).
- macOS handles the DualSense with its own framework, GameController, and puts it in extended report mode (0x31). In that mode the standard reports with buttons and axes stop arriving: gilrs sees the controller as connected, but nothing reaches it.
- SDL fixes this by reading those reports by hand. The most robust option on macOS is to read controllers with GameController, which knows the DualSense, DualShock 4, Xbox, Switch Pro and MFi.

Done:
- **`src/gamepads/apple.rs`** (macOS only, with `objc2-game-controller`):
  - Every frame, on the main thread (non-`Send` resource), it checks `GCController.controllers()`.
  - Each controller with a full profile (`extendedGamepad`) is passed to Bevy the way gilrs does it: connection, 17 buttons (A/B/X/Y by position, analogue triggers, D-pad, Start/Select/Home, stick clicks) and 4 axes. Only changes are sent.
- **No duplicates:** controllers read by GameController (by name) are not counted by gilrs. If gilrs already had them, they're disconnected on its side. While GameController has any, gilrs isn't recreated to search.
- **Verification:** there's no macOS here. The module, together with `gamepads.rs`, passes `cargo check` and `cargo clippy` with `--target aarch64-apple-darwin`, in a test crate with the Bevy pieces it uses (full Bevy can't be checked for macOS without its SDK). Nothing changes on Linux.
- **Still to do:** the user testing it on their Mac. The terminal should show "Mando: DualSense Wireless Controller (leído con GameController de macOS)" ("Controller: DualSense Wireless Controller (read with macOS GameController)").
