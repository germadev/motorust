# Performance Options and a Performance Log

## 1. Options for This Branch's Changes, and a Log of the Frame Rate (2026-10-16)

### 1.1 User Request
> "He notado una pérdida de rendimiento notable entre esta rama y main en 1P. En main ronda los 60~90 fps en un Mac M4 mini 16 GB, con los valores predeterminados. En esta rama 45~60 fps, además de que se produce un pop-in muy acusado. Lo he arrancado en ambos casos con `make up`. Estaba pensando en que puedes añadir una opción en gráficos que habilite o no los cambios que introduce esta rama respecto a main. Y si el programa genera un log, puedo jugar unas partidas cambiando de vez en cuando de modo para posteriormente poder pasarte los logs."

### 1.2 Implementation Details
- **What changes the drawing in one-player mode:** of this branch's changes, only the trees' levels of detail (`src/world/trees.rs`: every tree's distance worked out each frame and its mesh swapped as it crosses a bound, which Bevy then extracts and batches again; and the switch from the textured Meshy tree to its lighter mesh painted in its vertices, the pop-in seen). The split-screen detail does nothing with one player; the petals do less than before; the interface's scale, the tunes and the HUD's pop cost next to nothing. So the trees' levels of detail are the suspect, to be confirmed by the log.
- **Options > Graphics** (`src/settings.rs`, `src/options.rs`), each reset with the section, each saved, files without them loading with the defaults:
  - "Lighter far trees" (`lighter_far_trees`), **off by default**: off, every tree is drawn with its full mesh, as on `main`, and `dress_trees` leaves a whole tree alone (only a change of look, classic or new, touches it). On: as before, but from further away, 100 m and 280 m (50 m and 150 m with the screen split; they were 60/190 and 30/100), so the change is further off.
  - "Less detail for two players" (`lighter_split_screen`), on by default: off, `choose_detail` keeps `Detail::Full` with the screen split too.
  - "Performance log" (`performance_log`), on by default, not on the web.
- **The log** (`src/perf_log.rs`, `PerfLogPlugin`, in `Last`): each frame's real time noted; every 5 s, or at once when anything the stretch is noted with changes (the screen, the mode, one or two players, the pause, the track, the trees' look, the two options above, the FPS limit, the window's pixels and scale factor, the interface's scale), one line: `t=` (s since start), `frames`, `seconds`, `fps` (frames over their time), `fps_1pct_low` (the rate of the slowest 1 % of the frames, at least one), `ms_p50`, `ms_p95`, `ms_p99`, `ms_max`, the context, and `tree_levels=` (the trees in sight drawn whole/lighter/lightest). One file per session, `perf-<unix time>.log`, in a `logs` folder beside the settings (on macOS `~/Library/Application Support/motorust/logs`), headed with the version, the start time, the GPU (`RenderAdapterInfo`: name, backend, driver), the CPUs and the system; its path is printed when the game starts writing it. `world::Dressed` is public for the count.

### 1.3 Verification & Testing
- New test: `a_stretch_reads_as_its_rate_and_its_slowest_frames` (99 frames at 60 fps and one of 100 ms: 57.1 fps, the slowest 1 % at 10 fps, 16.67 ms in the middle, 100 ms the longest). The settings test loads a file without the new options with their defaults; the detail test now also checks that with "Less detail for two players" off two players get it all, and back.
- Run on lavapipe: the Graphics section shows the three options and fits; the log is written (`~/.config/motorust/logs/perf-<time>.log` there), its path printed, its heading naming the GPU, and a line every five seconds.
- `cargo test`: 445 passed. `cargo fmt --check` and `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings` clean.

## 2. More in the Log: CPU, Memory, the Simulation's Time, Resolution (2026-10-16)

### 2.1 User Request
> "En las pruebas también haré cambios de resolución. Añade todo lo que consideres en los logs; uso de CPU, memoria, etc. ¿Cómo ves añadir opciones de resolución dinámica? ¿FSR? ¿Alguna otra idea?"

### 2.2 Implementation Details
- **CPU and memory** (`src/main.rs`, `src/perf_log.rs`): with the log on when the game starts, Bevy's `SystemInformationDiagnosticsPlugin` (sysinfo, already in Bevy's default features, so no new dependency) reads them on another thread about once a second; each line has their averages: `cpu_process_pct` (the game's share of all the cores), `cpu_system_pct`, `mem_process_gib`, `mem_system_pct`.
- **The simulation's time:** `mark_frame_start` notes when each frame's update begins (`First`) and the log, in `Last`, how long it has taken: `update_ms_p50` and `update_ms_p95`. Far below the frame's time, the frame waits on drawing or the GPU; near it, the CPU holds it back. On lavapipe: 13-16 ms of 550-660 ms frames, GPU-bound as expected.
- **Entities** (`entities=`), and in the context the resolution option (`resolution=`), fullscreen and its kind (`fullscreen=`), `pipelined_rendering=` and `frame_latency=`; with the window's pixels and scale factor already there, a change of resolution starts a new line.
- **Dynamic resolution, FSR and other ideas** were answered, not built (see the reply): a render scale first, then a controller over it, a sharpening pass, and choices of anti-aliasing and shadows, decided on with the logs.

### 2.3 Verification & Testing
- The test of a stretch's figures now also reads the simulation's times. A run on lavapipe writes every field above.
- `cargo test`: 445 passed. `cargo fmt --check` and `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings` clean.
