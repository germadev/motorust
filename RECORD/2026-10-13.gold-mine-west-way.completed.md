## 2. Research: what West Way has

- Web searches only confirmed that West Way is one of the four tracks open from the start in *Moto Racer* (1997); the pages that might describe it (YouTube, MobyGames, the fandom wikis) refused automated fetching (402/403). The PS1 back cover on the Internet Archive shows a canyon with a stone arch, without naming the track. The PC CD image (Internet Archive, `moto-racer_pc_oem`) holds the tracks as `DATA/ILE0x.TRK` (raw floats, no names) and obfuscated `.BKF` banks; no loose `.3df` meshes, so `3df2obj` had nothing to convert there.
- The user's video settled it. Frames every 3 s (ffmpeg) and full-size stills of the key moments show, at dusk with Monument Valley buttes on the horizon:
  - a Western town at the start/finish (wooden false fronts with porches and balconies, a "FINISH" banner on two poles, ranch fences, a parked truck);
  - dunes, the road climbing and diving between them over blind crests, and esses along their slopes;
  - a gas station by the road on a crest (canopy, pumps, shop, tall sign);
  - oil pumpjacks on the tops of the dunes;
  - a narrow red canyon with sheer, overhanging walls and a tunnel through the rock;
  - a concrete highway viaduct the road passes under, with columns and retaining walls;
  - a blue-grey steel truss bridge, with a "CHECKPOINT" banner after it;
  - an adobe pueblo (low ochre houses with flat roofs, a dry tree) on a bend;
  - big diamond curve signs (green, yellow, red) with bent arrows, high on posts before the bends;
  - stone parapets along cliff edges, wooden fences and guard rails.
- The championship screen shows West Way's map: a lap of four laps of about 34 s each, constantly bending. Per the user, the lap need not be as short.

## 3. Implementation Details

### 3.1 The layout (`src/circuits/gold_mine.rs`, 5.95 km, was 4.79 km)

Designed in a scratchpad Python model of `src/track.rs` (centripetal Catmull-Rom, resampling, Hermite profiles, the branch heights, curvature, grades, crests, self-proximity, a rival-like speed model for the split times, the lake's shore with the game's noise, and the natural ground of the climbed massifs), then checked with the real tests.

- Before: 21° of turning per 100 m, 3 % of the lap tighter than 100 m and 22 % straighter than 1000 m; 92 m of climbing, made of long steady grades. After: 32°/100 m, 16 % tighter than 100 m, 14 % straighter than 1000 m (the start straight, the dunes straight and the bridge); 126 m of climbing, rolling almost everywhere (crests kept on the straights or gentle in the bends, hollows in the bends).
- In order: the town straight (0–340 m, its jump kept); the dunes (340–1000 m): a kink right, a straight over a blind crest at 595 m where the bike takes off, round a sharp left through a hollow; the red canyon (1000–1655 m): esses south between 36 m walls, rolling 11–23 m; the excavation (1655–2555 m, 22 m down to 5.5 m and up to 21 m); along the rim of the pit (2555–2930 m); the wooden bridge (2905–3425 m, crest at 3230 m); the switchbacks (3425–4580 m): along the lake's shore, a hairpin right and one left (radius about 50 m, moved 70 m east so they stay clear of the pit), the steel truss bridge over the gully (4200–4390 m), a long sweep onto the top (18 → 72 m, never under 3 %); over the top at 75 m (crossing the excavation's neck 70 m above it, at about 4780 m over 2100 m); down esses at up to 10 % past the pueblo into town.
- The mountain is now 78 m high (the road over the top is at 75 m); dunes of 15–18 m are climbed massifs round the road out of town.
- The splits (1706–2056 m and 2156–2504 m) fan out to 130 m from side to side: the lap swings ±26 m (right then left in the first, mirrored in the second); one branch runs straight on where the lap swings away, then curves out (up to 66 m from the lap), the other swings out before the lap does (65 m). Branch control points follow the lap's real centre line near the fork and join and the parametric shape in the middle. The rises are smoothed trapezoids built by integrating curvature (crests at most 0.0022/0.0016 1/m, hollows up to 0.007 1/m), fitted where the branch is 30 m or more from the lap: the high road +4.6 m, the deep road −4.2 m, the upper gallery +4.5 m, the lower gallery −4.2 m. Modelled split times: 5.13/5.12/5.09 s and 5.14/5.10/5.12 s.
- Banking: ×0.25 through the excavation (as before) and ×0.6 round the hairpins, so the verge beside them stays level with the road.
- Scenery moved to the new lap: arches at 0, 2640 and 4440 m (after the truss bridge, as in West Way); the trestle at 1625 m in the canyon; headframes at 2770 m and 4890 m; rails in town and along the rim; lantern rows; piles; billboards; stone parapets (`WallStyle::Stone`) along the rim and the switchbacks, timber elsewhere; the halls over the new splits.

### 3.2 Branch heights (`src/track.rs`)

- With a lap that winds, the point of the lap nearest to a far branch point jumps along it, and the branch inherited the lap's grades unevenly (bumps of 0.017 1/m in the model). Now, once its walls have parted from the lap's, a branch without a loop goes over, between 23 m and 68 m from the lap's centre line (`FORK_FAR_BLEND` 45 m), from the height of the lap's surface beside it to the height of the lap as far along it as it counts as (`counts_as`), which runs on steadily. Where the two share the road nothing changes.

### 3.3 Open pits cut into higher ground (`src/world/terrain.rs`)

- Where the ground round a pit is higher than its rim, the benches now go on up into it a step at a time instead of a vertical cut. The raised mountain made a cliff of up to 44 m (with a rock tower) right at the rim, and the terrain grid blended it into the top bench.

### 3.4 Bridges with their own style, and steel trusses (`src/world/scenery.rs`, `structures.rs`, `road.rs`, `terrain.rs`)

- `Scenery::bridges` is now `&[Bridge { span, style }]` (the old `bridge_style` field is gone); `BridgeStyle::Truss` joins `RedArches` and `Wooden`. The concrete deck is shared (`concrete_deck`, with piers where asked); a truss bridge adds two through trusses (top chord over sloping end posts, posts every 3 points, diagonals sloping down towards the middle) braced overhead, in weathered blue-grey steel, with piers every 24 points, and concrete walls on the deck.

### 3.5 West Way's roadside (`src/world/desert.rs`, new; `Scenery::desert`)

- `Desert { gas_stations, pumpjacks, overpasses, pueblos, curve_signs }`, one vertex-coloured mesh per building:
  - gas station: forecourt slab, canopy on four columns with a yellow and red fascia, two islands of pumps, a shop with door and window, and a tall sign with "GAS" drawn by `sign_image`;
  - pumpjacks: frame, samson post, gear box and well head; the walking beam with its horse's head (`Nod`) and the crank with its counterweights (`Crank`), both driven by `desert::pump` from the elapsed time so they stay in step (beam angle 0.28 · sin(crank angle), 1.4 rad/s); meshes shared;
  - viaduct: a 2 m deep concrete deck 13 m wide from rock wall to rock wall (±29.5 m), parapets, a pair of columns and a cap beam beyond each wall;
  - pueblo: two rows of adobe houses in the pueblo's own frame (three ochres, parapets, roof beams sticking out at the front, a door and windows framed in blue, half of them with a set-back upper floor and a ladder), and a dry tree;
  - diamond signs: a 128 px picture drawn per colour and direction (green/white, yellow/black, red/white; a pale border; an arrow up and bending the way of the bend), alpha-masked, double-sided, 3.2 m across, centred 6.2 m up a post on the outside of the bend.
- Gold Mine: a gas station on the right of the dunes' crest (600 m), six pumpjacks on the dunes, the viaduct across the canyon 14 m up (1300 m), the pueblo on the outside of the bend at 5450 m, and eleven diamond signs in place of its chevrons (red before the hairpins and the dunes' sharp left).
- Town houses: `Houses::style` (`HouseStyle::{Cottage, FalseFront}`); Gold Mine's are false fronts standing up over the roof with a cornice, and a porch (a balcony on two floors) on posts.

### 3.6 Tests

- New: `desert::tests::{a_pumpjack_s_beam_rises_and_falls_once_a_turn_of_its_crank, diamond_signs_point_the_way_of_the_bend, the_desert_s_works_stand_clear_of_the_roads_on_the_ground, viaducts_run_high_over_the_road_into_rock_on_both_sides, curve_signs_stand_before_bends_their_way}`; `structures::concrete_bridges_have_a_deck_out_to_the_walls` (was red bridges only) covers the truss; `an_open_pit_steps_down_to_the_water_bench_by_bench` checks benches going on up into higher ground; the mountain verge test leaves out bridges as well as the excavation.

## 4. Follow-up: a wooden bridge narrower than the road

### 4.1 User Request
- "El puente de madera es muy ancho, debería ser más estrecho que la carretera" (the wooden bridge is very wide; it should be narrower than the road).

### 4.2 Implementation Details
- `src/track.rs`: `Layout::narrows: &[Narrowing { from, to, wall }]`. The lap fills its `walls` with `WALL_DISTANCE`, closing in to `wall` over the `NARROW_EASE` (25 m) before the stretch and opening out over as many after it (smoothstep). `Track::half_width_at(index)` (and `half_width_beside`) is the asphalt's half width short of the walls by `NARROW_MARGIN` (0.5 m) where they are closer than it: the grass check, where a fallen rider rejoins and the rivals' lines (`Waypoint::half_width` in `src/bike/ai.rs`) keep inside the narrow stretch. Collisions already use the walls (`wall_at`, via the edges). Every other track has `narrows: &[]`, so nothing changes on them.
- Gold Mine: the wooden bridge narrows to fences 6 m from the middle (12 m between them, against 14 m of asphalt and 23 m between the walls elsewhere).
- `src/world/structures.rs`: the wooden bridge's planks, fences and piers follow the walls point by point. `src/world/road.rs`: no asphalt, paint or curbs under the planks of a wooden bridge (they would show past a fence narrower than the road).

### 4.3 Verification & Testing
- New test `track::walls_close_in_on_a_narrow_bridge_and_open_out_after_it`. The flat-out lap now checks the bike against the solid edge where it is (`edge_beside`) instead of the usual wall distance; the terrain edge test accepts edges as close as the walls where they close in. The rival, fork and pack tests pass with the narrow bridge (rivals keep their lines inside it).

## 5. Follow-up: cacti and tumbleweeds

### 5.1 User Request
- "Añade algún cactus y bolas de paja" (add some cacti and tumbleweeds).

### 5.2 Implementation Details
- `Desert::cacti: Cacti { count, spans }` and `Desert::tumbleweeds: &[Tumbleweed { along, reach }]` (`src/world/scenery.rs`).
- `src/world/desert.rs`: three saguaro shapes (a ribbed trunk 4–6.5 m with one to three arms bending up, faces alternately in two greens), shared meshes; placed pseudo-randomly along the spans either side, 15.5–75 m out (more of them nearer), on level dry ground clear of every road, of the other works and of each other, turned and sized at random. Tumbleweeds: a ball of 70 dry twigs (one shared mesh) rolling across the road on a way worked out at load (the ground every metre, the asphalt where it is on the road, and a hop of 1.4 m over the walls), bouncing, slanting a little with the wind, and shrinking away at either end before starting again (`Tumbling`, moved by `desert::tumble`).
- Gold Mine: 90 cacti along the town, the dunes, the rim of the pit, the climb, the top and the descent; eight tumbleweeds crossing the road in town, in the dunes, along the rim of the pit, over the top and on the way down.

### 5.3 Verification & Testing
- New tests `desert::tests::{cacti_stand_on_level_dry_ground_clear_of_the_roads, tumbleweeds_roll_across_the_road_and_hop_over_its_walls}`.
