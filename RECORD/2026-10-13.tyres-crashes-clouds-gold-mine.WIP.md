## 2. Implementation Details

### 2.1 Round tyres with tread patterns (`src/bike/model/tyre.rs`, `imported.rs`, `mod.rs`)
- The Meshy wheels carried their tyres as low-poly rings: measured from the meshes, their outline wanders by about 2 cm round the wheel (some have whole 10° sectors with no vertex in the outer 15 %), and some have smudges of the bodywork's colours.
- `tyre::strip` cuts every triangle of a wheel mesh whose centroid is beyond 0.63 of the radius, or that reaches beyond 0.67 (the spikes where rim and tyre met), and measures the old tyre's half width (beyond 0.7 of the radius, × 0.95).
- `tyre::mesh` is a surface of revolution round the axle, 96 segments by 32: a closed cross-section of two half superellipses (exponent 2.6 over the crown, 4 towards the rim, so the sidewall stays full down to the bead at 0.6 of the radius). Normals are analytic; tangents generated for the normal map. Texture coordinates repeat 18 times round; across, the tread maps to the middle band (±75° from the crown) and the sidewalls to the rest.
- `tyre::images` paints, per pattern, a 128 × 256 colour texture (rubber grain, a slightly scrubbed crown, darker grooves, a coloured stripe round the sidewalls) and a normal map from the groove depth field. Patterns: `Slick` (no grooves, red stripe), `Sport` (staggered lightning-bolt grooves, yellow), `Touring` (centre groove and chevrons, blue), `Sipe` (long sweeping sipes, white). Grooves are polylines with distances in true proportions and anti-aliased edges; the pattern wraps seamlessly. Four shared materials; `Tread::of(Design)` gives each imported bike its pattern (two bikes each).

### 2.2 Tumbling crashes and spinning wheels (`src/bike/crash.rs`, `src/bike/model/mod.rs`)
- The fallen bike rolls over its real outline (`OUTLINE`: tyres, shoulders, footpegs, fairing, bar ends, mirrors, screen, nose, tail). `lift(pose)` is the height that keeps the lowest point of the outline on the road; the centre of mass height `center_height` along the roll is the potential: on the ground the roll rate changes by `-g · dh/dθ / k²` (k² = 0.3 m²), with kinetic friction (3 rad/s² above 1 rad/s) and damping (1.5/s). The lowest centre of mass is lying on a side at 1.3 rad (bar end and footpeg), matching `DOWN_LEAN`; upright and upside down are unstable.
- At the fall the roll starts at `2 + 0.35·jolt + 0.09·(speed − 20)` rad/s (at most 11), and a hard hit throws the bike up (`0.25·(jolt − 4) + 0.04·(speed − 25)` m/s, at most 3). Rolling onto a side faster than 5 rad/s bounces it up (0.3 m/s per rad/s, keeping 75 % of the roll); hard landings bounce back 30 % (at most 1.5 m/s). In the air it turns about its centre of mass, which falls ballistically, and lands on its outline. Stopped head on by more than 8 m/s, it is thrown up (0.45 m/s per m/s, 4.5–6 m/s) and flips once end over end over its front wheel during the flight; on the ground the pitch settles to a whole turn.
- Tuned in a Python model first: a 12 m/s fall just lays the bike down; 45 m/s rolls it about ¾ of a turn; 60 m/s with a big jolt 1–3 turns with a couple of bounces; always ending on a side.
- Drag only applies on the ground; in the air the bike flies on.
- The wheels keep the bike's speed at the fall (also against a wall) and spin down (0.35/s and 0.8 m/s²); `spin_wheels` uses them while the rider is down (`Wheel::front`).

### 2.3 Realistic clouds (`src/world/sky.rs`)
- The zeno vector outlines are gone. Six cumulus shapes (four heaped, one wide stratocumulus, one small) are painted at startup into 512 × 256 images: a field of thickness from balls seen through (puffs along a dome plus smaller bubbles round their tops and towers), joined with a smooth union, warped and made billowy by noise, cut flat at a slightly ragged base. Opacity is `1 − e^(−16·t)`; light is the transmittance of a 14-step march towards the sun over the thickness field (absorption 22) times the facing of the lumps (the thickness gradient over ±4 px as relief), blending a blue-grey shadow, a darker base and sunlit white. Two veils of cirrus (combed fibres in bands) sit higher up, faint.
- 18 cumulus quads and 6 cirrus quads, each with its own material (opacity fading lower clouds into the haze); quads with the sun on their right are mirrored so the light comes from the sun's side. Startup cost stays small (one evaluation of the field per pixel plus lookups); runtime cost unchanged (a few blended quads).

### 2.4 Light per track: day and dusk (`src/world/sky.rs`, `src/world/mod.rs`, `scenery.rs`)
- `SkyStyle::{Day, Dusk}` in each `Scenery`; `Palette::of(style)` holds the sky's colours, a glow round the sun, the sun's place and colour, sunlight colour and strength, sky light and cloud colour. Day keeps exactly the previous look.
- `light_the_sky` applies it when the circuit changes (and to new cameras' fog): a new dome mesh (the old one's data is only on the GPU), fog and clear colour, sun disc, sunlight direction/colour/illuminance, ambient light, cloud tint and which way each cloud is lit.
- Dusk: violet zenith, rose above the horizon, orange horizon glowing round a low sun in the west-north-west (elevation 0.2 rad), warm sunlight at 80 %, purple ambient, peach clouds.

### 2.5 The Gold Mine (`src/circuits/gold_mine.rs`, `src/world/mine.rs`, `structures.rs`, `terrain.rs`, `road.rs`)
- Layout designed with a Python preview of the game's centripetal Catmull-Rom: 2.2 km clockwise, tightest corner 70 m, stretches at least 88 m apart. Elevation with a crest in town (~200 m), the climb to the mesa and gallery (up to 16.5 m), the canyon, and down to the river.
- `Soil::Mine`: dry red earth (sand texture, red vertex colours) and banded red/rust/ochre rock (`canyon_vertex_color`). `WallStyle::Timber` (planks). River and lake via `Coast::Function` under a wooden bridge. Mesas and buttes, canyon cuttings, flat-topped red mesas on the horizon.
- Mine galleries (tunnels in `Soil::Mine`): an irregular vault of rock (straight walls to 4.5 m, a vault to a 9.5 m crown, bulging out up to 1.2 m by noise above bike height), shaded per quad by the lanterns (bright and warm by every other set, darker up in the vault); timber sets every 8 m the shape of the vault (posts, rafters, cap); lanterns hanging from the rafters; plank walkways; arched mouths in the rock face with a heavy timber set.
- `world::mine`: rail lines (gravel bed, sleepers, steel rails, one mesh per line) with trains of ore carts that go up and down them smoothly (at most 4 m/s); a timber truss trestle over the road from rock to rock with a cart crossing; headframes (tapering timber tower, back struts, winding house, cables) with two winding wheels turning (`Spin`); lanterns on posts; piles of crates, barrels and dynamite, and ore heaps with gold nuggets. Carts and lanterns share meshes and materials.
- The song: "Toll of the Iron Mile" (`assets/music/gold_mine.mp3`, −11.9 LUFS measured with ffmpeg's ebur128).

### 2.6 Smoother crests on Bahía GP and Loop Island (no change to the bike)
- Diagnosis with rivals on three lines and a flat-out rider: in the air a bike neither turns nor leans (traction is `AIR_STEERING`), so a takeoff in a bend straightens it. Loop Island took off at 1410 m in a 150 m bend (the S-bends crest) and over the shore jump at 1775 m, landing in a 130 m bend; Bahía's start jump landed at 240 m at the entry of a kink (radius ~620 m).
- Bahía GP: the start jump moved 40 m earlier (crest at 155 m), landing at about 195 m on the straight. Loop Island: a long, gentle rise over the S-bends (1360–1480 m) and a gently rolling shore road (no jump). Bahía renamed "Bahía GP" (both languages).
- Test helpers that put bikes at height 0 near Bahía's start now put them on the road.

### 2.8 Smooth track loading (`src/world/mod.rs`, `road.rs`, `structures.rs`, `props.rs`, `lettering.rs`, `sea.rs`, `terrain.rs`)
- Measured (dev profile) the main-thread work when a prepared circuit is put in the world: up to ~535 ms in a single frame (Turbo Bay), mostly tree placement (32 000 candidate spots, each looking for the road), road meshes, tunnel meshes and the signs' lettering.
- Now prepared on the background thread with the terrain: the meshes and materials of the roads, tunnels and bridges (`road_pieces`, `structure_pieces`), the tree plan (`plan_trees`, in planting order, the buildings checked when planting, with the same result as before) and the tree models, the signs' images (`sign_image` keeps every sign it draws, shared across circuits), and the shore map's image.
- Each tree variant is merged into one mesh (bark coloured in its vertices), so a tree is one entity instead of about six: fewer entities to spawn and to draw.
- Background preparation works the terrain on fewer threads (half the cores minus one), so the menu keeps its frame rate.
- The frame that adds a circuit dropped to 10–22 ms in the dev profile. (Bevy's `RenderAssetBytesPerFrame` was tried and dropped: in 0.19 deferred meshes never got their buffers and were not drawn.)
