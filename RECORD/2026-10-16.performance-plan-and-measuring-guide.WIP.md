### 1.3 Priorities (the rules for every later change)
1. Frame rate first: an arcade racer must stay smooth.
2. No pop-in: nothing may appear, vanish or change look all at once in view. A cheaper setting that pops is worse than a dearer one that does not.
3. No hitches: no change in the middle of a race that makes new pipelines (things vanish while they are made, and the frame stutters).
4. Every change measurable on the user's machine (the log and the benchmark), and switchable off in Options > Graphics so it can be compared with `main`.

### 1.5 Diagnosis of the 1P Drop and Pop-In (to be confirmed by measuring)
- In one-player mode, the only change of the branch that alters the drawing was the trees' levels of detail: every tree's distance worked out each frame and its mesh swapped across a bound (Bevy extracts and batches it again), and the textured Meshy tree swapped for a vertex-painted lighter mesh at 60 m (the pop-in seen). It is now off by default.
- The shadows' pop-in was Bevy's: past the far bound of the last cascade it draws no shadow at all, so every shadow came in at once at 250 m. Fixed by the fade, at any level.
- Not yet measured on the user's machine with the new defaults. `make up` builds the game's own code at `opt-level = 1`; comparisons are fairer with `make build` on both branches.

### 1.6 Questions Asked and Answers Given
- **Dynamic antialiasing:** not built. Changing MSAA rebuilds every mesh pipeline; Bevy draws nothing whose pipeline is not ready, so things vanish for a few frames and the frame stutters at each change, worse than the edges it would save. Made a fixed option instead (MSAA 4×, FXAA, none).
- **Other settings lowered with the frame rate:** only the shadows, which change with no new pipeline (cascade count and distance are uniform data, the shadow map's size a texture). The grass, flowers and trees' distances were left out of the automatic levels: moving them in a race is a pop-in.
- **Shadows far away with less detail:** a first version pushed the last cascade to 500 m, coarse; dropped for the fade, which removes the pop at any distance without drawing more casters.
- **Dynamic resolution and FSR (recommended order, none built yet):**
  1. A render scale (for example 100, 85, 70, 50 %): draw the 3D at less than the window's pixels, scale it up with Bevy's contrast adaptive sharpening (the sharpening half of FSR 1), the interface at full resolution on top. The biggest win on Retina and scaled 4K Mac displays, where "Dynamic" resolution draws very many pixels (the log's `window=` shows how many). Changing the size makes no new pipeline, only textures, so it suits automatic control.
  2. Then a controller over that scale, as the shadows' one, to hold the target frame rate.
  3. FSR 1 (spatial, EASU and RCAS) ported to a shader, if scaling up with sharpening is not enough. FSR 2/3 (temporal) need motion vectors and jitter and have no Bevy integration; MetalFX is not reachable through wgpu.
  4. Other ideas: Bevy's experimental GPU occlusion culling (hills hide many trees), tree impostors (billboards) instead of lighter meshes far away (less pop), a draw-distance option.
  The note in Bevy 0.19: `MainPassResolutionOverride` only shrinks the main pass inside a full-size target for an upscaler (DLSS) to fill, so a render scale needs the 3D cameras to draw into a smaller texture shown over the window, split screen included.

### 1.7 How to Measure
- **The performance log** (Options > Graphics > Performance log, on by default; the path is printed when the game starts): `~/Library/Application Support/motorust/logs/perf-<unix time>.log` on macOS. A heading with the GPU, CPUs and system; then a line every 5 s, and at once when the screen, mode, players, pause, track, resolution, window or an option changes, so no line mixes two of them. Fields: `t`, `frames`, `seconds`, `fps`, `fps_1pct_low`, `ms_p50`, `ms_p95`, `ms_p99`, `ms_max`, `update_ms_p50`, `update_ms_p95` (the simulation's share of each frame: far below the frame time means the GPU or drawing holds it back), `cpu_process_pct`, `cpu_system_pct`, `mem_process_gib`, `mem_system_pct`, `entities`, and the context: `state`, `mode`, `players`, `paused`, `track`, `trees`, `lighter_far_trees`, `lighter_split_screen`, `shadows` (chosen/in use), `antialiasing`, `fps_limit`, `resolution`, `fullscreen`, `pipelined_rendering`, `frame_latency`, `window` (physical pixels @ scale factor), `ui_scale`, `tree_levels` (trees in sight drawn whole/lighter/lightest).
- **The benchmark:** `make bench` (`cargo run --release -- --bench`; or `MOTORUST_BENCH=1`). Free ride on autopilot, frame rate unlimited, eight scenarios per track: `1p` (shadows high, MSAA 4×, whole trees), `1p-shadows-medium`, `1p-shadows-low`, `1p-fxaa`, `1p-no-antialiasing`, `1p-lighter-far-trees`, `2p` (less detail), `2p-full-detail`. Each settles 4 s and is measured 10 s (`MOTORUST_BENCH_SECONDS`), on the tracks in `MOTORUST_BENCH_TRACKS` (as `0,2`) or all five (about 12 minutes). The table is printed and written to `bench-<unix time>.txt` beside the log; the game then closes. The window and resolution are the player's own, so run it at each resolution to compare; the settings it tries are never saved.
- **For a Claude session on the user's Mac:** from the repository, `make bench` (or `MOTORUST_BENCH_TRACKS=0 MOTORUST_BENCH_SECONDS=8 make bench` for a quick pass), then read the newest `bench-*.txt` and `perf-*.log` in `~/Library/Application Support/motorust/logs/`. Compare `1p` against the other 1P rows to see what each setting costs, `update_ms` against `ms_p50` in the log to tell CPU from GPU, and `2p` against `2p-full-detail` for the split screen's savings. To compare with `main`, which has neither the log nor the benchmark, play the same track on both builds (`make build`) with the options in the "Same as `main`" column above and compare the FPS counter, or the log's `1p` lines on this branch.

## 2. Stopping the Benchmark, Following It in the Terminal, Results per Machine (2026-10-16)

### 2.1 User Requests

> "Me vendría bien que `make bench` vaya mostrando información en el terminal, por si te quiero preguntar algo. Como lo ejecutaré en varias máquinas, ¿dónde vas a guardar los resultados?"

### 2.2 Implementation Details
- No alias: `make bench` stays the command (a `test.performance` alias was added and taken out again at the user's word). Its help line says Esc stops it.
- **In the terminal** (`src/bench.rs`, plain lines, apart from the game's log): first the machine (`motorust <version> benchmark on <machine>`; the system, CPU and cores, memory, from Bevy's `SystemInfo`; the GPU, backend and driver; the window's pixels and scale factor, the resolution option and fullscreen; the trees, pipelined rendering and frames queued), how many scenarios, how long each and about how long in all, where the results go and that Esc stops it. Then, for each scenario, `[ n/N] track · scenario`: "setting up the race" when a track or the players change, "settling 4 s, then measuring 10 s", and when measured its figures: fps, slowest 1 %, the frame time at the middle and at 95 %, the simulation's time at the middle (`FrameStart::elapsed`, the same measure as the log's `update_ms`) and the game's CPU (Bevy's sysinfo reading, as a share of all the cores), with about how many minutes are left. At the end, the whole table and where it is written.
- **Where the results go:** `bench-results/` in the folder it is run from (the repository with `make bench`; if it cannot be made there, beside the performance log). One file for each run, named after when it began (UTC, `stamp`: `2026-10-16_1830`) and the machine's name (`hostname`, made fit for a file name by `file_name`: `joshuas-mac-mini`), e.g. `2026-10-16_1830_joshuas-mac-mini.txt`: its heading is the machine's lines (as `#` comments), then the table's heading, each scenario's line written and flushed as soon as it is measured (track, scenario, fps, 1 % low, p50 ms, p95 ms, sim ms, cpu %), and `# finished` or `# stopped after N of M`. The run's performance log goes beside it (`<same name>.perf.log`, through `PerfLog::to`). Runs on several machines sit side by side, to be committed and pushed, and read from the branch. (This replaces section 1.7's `bench-<unix time>.txt` beside the performance log.)
- **Stopping:** Esc writes `# stopped after N of M`, prints the table so far and closes; closing the window or Ctrl+C keep every scenario already written.
- A file left in the repository by a crash during a benchmark run here (`motorust-crash.log`, committed by mistake in `92f0579` and removed by the user's `c4332b4`) is now ignored by git.

### 4.2 What the Runs Say
- **GPU-bound.** The simulation takes 1.2–4.4 ms of frames of 7–17 ms: the rest is drawing 5.7 million pixels. One player: 92–143 fps; two: 57–90 fps. Ghost Island is the heaviest (1P 92 fps, 2P with all the detail 57 fps).
- **Reproducible.** The tracks both runs measured (Bahía GP, Turbo Bay, Loop Island) came out within a few per cent of each other, row by row.
- **But the settings cannot be read apart from the road.** On the same track and players, each scenario carried on the same race, so each measured a different stretch of road, and the road moves the frame rate as much as the settings do: within one scenario (Mina Dorada, shadows low) the rate went from 122 to 104 fps from one five-second line of the log to the next, and the run has Mina Dorada faster with medium shadows (116.9) than with low (105.0), and Ghost Island's lighter far trees slower than anything else. Since the order is fixed, the bias repeats from run to run, so being reproducible does not make the comparisons fair. What holds across all five tracks: two players with less detail run 18–34 % faster than with all of it; and the first scenario of each track (MSAA, high shadows) is the slowest one-player row.
- **Entities "doubling"** (6464 on the first track, 12928 from the second on): the log printed `Entities::len`, the entity indices ever allocated (the old world and the new one were alive together while the track changed), not the entities alive.
- **CPU and memory of the game at 0 on macOS:** Bevy 0.19 builds `sysinfo` on macOS with its `apple-app-store` feature (→ `apple-sandbox`), under which `refresh_processes_specifics` reads no process at all, so `process(pid)` is never found and both readings fall back to 0. The system's CPU and memory are read fine.
- **The stop at 19/40:** with no one touching the Mac for minutes, the display went to sleep, its monitor was removed, the window with it, and Bevy closes when no windows are left.

### 4.3 Implementation Details
- **The game's own CPU and memory** (`src/perf_log.rs`): the CPU time the game has had (`getrusage(RUSAGE_SELF)`, user and system, on Linux and macOS) read when a stretch begins (`CpuClock::start`) and when it ends (`CpuClock::share`): its share of all the cores over the stretch (`cpu_share`: CPU seconds / wall seconds / cores × 100, as Bevy's figure was meant). On macOS, the memory is read with `proc_pid_rusage(RUSAGE_INFO_V2)`'s `ri_phys_footprint`, what Activity Monitor shows. Elsewhere, and on Windows for the CPU, Bevy's readings as before (`process_cpu`). `libc` 0.2 is a direct dependency on Unix (the version Bevy already has in the lock file).
- **The entities alive:** `Entities::count_spawned` (a pass over 13 000 entries every five seconds).
- **The benchmark** (`src/bench.rs`): every scenario sets the race up again (`apply` no longer says whether to), so all of them ride the same first stretch of the track and differ only in what they try; the first scenario is ridden once before, unmeasured ("warming up 14 s, not measured", then "setting up the race again"), while the shaders are compiled and the caches fill; the CPU column is the game's share over the measured ten seconds (`CpuClock`), Bevy's reading only where that cannot be had. The heading says so and its time estimate counts setting up each race (`SETTING_UP`, 2.5 s) and the warm-up.
- **`make bench` on macOS** runs under `caffeinate -dis` (`KEEP_AWAKE` in the `Makefile`, empty elsewhere), so the display, the disk and the system stay awake for the run.
- Docs: `docs/architecture.md` (bench and perf log rows), `docs/building.md` (`make bench`).

## 6. The M4 Pro's Runs; Resolution in the Benchmark: the Render Scale (2026-10-15)

### 6.2 What the Runs Say (whole laps, `8f70d15`, 3024 × 1898 at 2×, fullscreen Desktop)
- **M1 Pro** (macOS 26.3.1): one player 94–125 fps as the game starts, two players 70–93; Ghost Island the heaviest. Against `1p`, track by track (Bahía, Turbo Bay, Loop Island, Mina Dorada, Ghost Island): shadows medium +6/+16/+4/+10/+15 %, low +13/+16/+9/+16/+29 %; FXAA +4/+9/+9/+5/+15 %; no antialiasing +16/+22/+12/+16/+31 %; lighter far trees +3/+9/+3/−2/+12 %; two players with less detail +26–30 % over all the detail.
- **M4 Pro in Low Power Mode** (macOS 27.0.1, `77a3e5c`): about the M1 Pro's rates, with far worse slowest 1 % (17–72 fps) and the simulation 1.4–2.2 times slower than on the M1 Pro (Mina Dorada 2P 7.24 ms against 3.30): the CPU and GPU held back, as the user confirmed. Not to be compared.
- **M4 Pro at full power** (`65ec47c`): one player 149–241 fps, two 112–155; 1.5–1.9 times the M1 Pro (1.93 on Bahía GP). Against `1p`: shadows low +12–19 %, no antialiasing +11–22 %, FXAA +6–12 %, lighter far trees 0–9 %; two players with less detail +29–36 %. The simulation is 0.5–3.6 ms of frames of 3.4–11.7 ms: on Mina Dorada and Ghost Island about half of a 1P frame, so there the CPU starts to count.
- No run changed the resolution: the benchmark draws at the player's window (5.7 million pixels on these Retina screens), and the screen did not say so.

### 6.3 Implementation Details
- **The render scale** (`src/render_scale.rs`, new; section 1.6's first recommendation): Options > Graphics > "Escala de resolución" / "Resolution scale", 100 % (the default), 85, 70 or 50 % of each side of the window (`RENDER_SCALES`, `Settings::render_scale`, saved; the row says the pixels, "70 % (2117 × 1329)"). Under 100 %, the 3D cameras draw into an image that size (`render_size`, at least a quarter of each side), a new image for each size; the interface's camera (the only 2D one, already drawing over the whole window at order 100) clears the window, draws that image as a sprite as big as the window (`ScaledWorld`, linear sampling) and sharpens it with Bevy's `ContrastAdaptiveSharpening` (0.5), which runs before the UI pass, so the interface is drawn on top at the window's resolution, unsharpened. At 100 % the 3D cameras draw on the window and the interface's camera over them, as always (no clear, no sharpening, no sprite). Bevy sizes a camera again only when its window, image or projection changes, not its target, so the projections are marked changed when the target is (as `follow_window_size` does for the window). Split screen's viewports are halves of what the world is drawn on (`camera::update_camera_viewports`). While the world is drawn smaller the interface's camera draws on its own, without multisampling (`world::detail::antialias`); the world's cameras keep the antialiasing chosen. No new pipeline for the world on a change (only the sharpening's and the interface's without MSAA, the first time), so it can be changed in a race.
- **In the benchmark** (`src/bench.rs`): `Scenario::render_scale`; three new scenarios, `1p-render-70`, `1p-render-50` and `2p-render-70` (less detail at 70 %): eight settings on the 1P lap, three on the 2P one, the table's columns as before. On the screen, a line with the pixels the world is drawn at under the scenario's name (`resolution_said`: "3D 2117 × 1329 · 70 %", or "3D 3024 × 1898 · 100 %").
- **In the performance log:** `render_scale=70 render=2117x1329` after `antialiasing`.
- Docs: `docs/gameplay.md` (Options > Graphics), `docs/architecture.md` (the module, the benchmark), `docs/building.md` (`make bench`).

### 6.4 Verification & Testing
- New tests: `the_world_is_drawn_at_a_share_of_each_side_of_the_window` and `under_100_percent_the_world_is_drawn_into_an_image_shown_over_the_window` (a Bevy app with a 2000 × 1000 window: at 70 % the 3D camera draws into a 1400 × 700 image, the sprite shows it, the interface's camera clears and sharpens; back at 100 %, on the window, no clear, no sharpening, the sprite hidden). The benchmark's tests take the three new scenarios (eleven a track, one change from the first but for two players at 70 %) and the 3D line. `cargo test`: 469 passed (with the HUD's changes of the same commit); `cargo clippy --all-targets`: no warnings; `cargo fmt --check`: clean.
- Seen on lavapipe (1280 × 720, a short benchmark of Bahía GP): at 50 % the world drawn at 640 × 360, softer, with the HUD, minimap, speedometer and label sharp; two players at 70 %, each half drawn at 896 × 504 in its place; the label's 3D line for every scenario. The frame rates mean nothing there.
